# The Five-Dollar Bike

**Recreation / HUS · idea for selection, not an approved script · about 80 seconds**

A kid takes gift wrapping seriously because his best friend can name any present without opening it. A five-dollar school gift swap gives them one last round. Real paper, tape, a bike and a straight-faced narrator; no game mechanics.

| Beat | What we see |
|---|---|
| 1. The five-dollar limit | **HUS, HUS, HUS:** a fold, a crease, a strip of tape across a bike bell. It still *dings*. By second two the wrapped bike rolls under **GIFT SWAP · $5 LIMIT**. “Where’d you get a bike for five bucks?” Rewind on the bell. |
| 2. Last year | His friend guessed “mug” on sight. Cut to the friend drinking cocoa out of it. The handle is still gift-wrapped. |
| 3. Practice partner | While his friends go out, the kid sets up a paper roll at the kitchen table. His little brother gets a cracker for every correct guess. Two columns on the fridge: **GIFT / GUESS**. So far they match. |
| 4. The first speed trial | He wraps a folding umbrella. One button sticks through the paper. His brother presses it. “That was my first present that could unwrap itself.” |
| 5. A useful wrong answer | Neater folds only make the outline easier to guess. He changes the outline instead. Two empty oatmeal tubs and a paper tube make a convincing dumbbell. His brother braces to lift it with both arms. Inside: one tea bag. First cracker the kid gets to keep. |
| 6. The swap | A line forms at his wrapping table. His old friend walks straight past it to the giant bike-shaped present. He flicks the bell through the paper. “Bike.” He doesn’t even look at the tag. |
| 7. The gift | The tag says **OPEN HERE** and points to the little bow on the handlebars. Under it: socks, price sticker **$4.99**. The friend holds the socks. The kid clips on his helmet and pedals home on the still-wrapped bike, red bow bobbing down the street. Cut there. |

**Muted test:** can a viewer read the limit, mistake the wrapping for the gift, then see both who keeps the socks and who keeps the bicycle? No final line explaining the trick. If selected, write the timed narration for approval before building shots or recording voices.
