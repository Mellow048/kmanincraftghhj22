# The One-Trip Grocery Run

**Recreation / HUS · idea for selection, not an approved script · about 80 seconds**

A kid turns carrying all the groceries from the car in one trip into an absurd athletic discipline. It pays off when a neighbor is stuck holding the apartment door with both hands full. Ordinary grocery bags, cans, a real stairwell and a dead-serious narrator; no game mechanics. The joke is the tiny item he still has to make a second trip for.

| Beat | What we see |
|---|---|
| 1. One trip | **HUS, HUS, HUS:** the kid speed-walks from a real car with grocery bags looped over both arms, two totes, a paper-towel roll balanced on his head. An orange slips; his elbow catches it against a bag. Text: **ONE TRIP.** “Why carry all of that at once?” Rewind on the orange. |
| 2. The rule | His sister makes three tidy trips between the car and the kitchen. He grabs every bag she left. The handles bite his fingers. He does not put a single one down. |
| 3. First practice | He hangs empty bags from a broom handle and tests a balanced grip. Cans make one side sag. He swaps them; the other side sags. His sister calmly takes one grocery bag inside. |
| 4. The obstacle course | A chair becomes a narrow doorway. A towel becomes the stair rail. He tries opening the real front door with his foot while eggs roll in their carton. First the door wins. Then the carton nearly does. |
| 5. The method | He weighs bags on a bathroom scale, places the heavy ones close, and practices the route with cans and a carton of eggs. One bag handle tears. He catches each rolling can against the wall, resets, and tries again. |
| 6. The real test | Rain. His neighbor has grocery bags in both hands and cannot reach the apartment doorbell. The kid steps in, gathers the bags in one huge, carefully balanced load, and uses his elbow to ring. The neighbor opens the door. |
| 7. One left | He gets every bag inside in a single trip. Both neighbors look at the cleared car. One lone potato is still on the back seat. He goes back for it with grave purpose, holding it in one hand: **HUS.** Cut on the second trip. |

**Muted test:** can a viewer see the skill, understand the one-trip rule, and spot the potato left behind before the hero does? The repeated grocery hauls are the HUS actions; breaths mark each visible rep. No voiceover explains the final joke. If selected, write the timed narration for approval before building shots or recording voices.
