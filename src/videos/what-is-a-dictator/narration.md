# Narration — What Is a Dictator

**Status: DRAFT. Not approved, not recorded.** Nothing is built until the owner approves the script.

Topic: `Topics/Explainer`. Read `Topics/Explainer/SCRIPT.md` before changing a word. The reference film, to build
like and never to retell: `Topics/Explainer/reference/bread/`. The owner gave the title (2026-11-13): "what is a
dictator?" for explainer.

**The question:** What is a dictator?

**The answer, in one sentence a nine-year-old could repeat:** A dictator is a ruler nobody is allowed to tell no:
not a judge, not the people, not a vote.

**The thing everybody knows:** the word **no**. Being told it, and saying it. In the film it is a red speech
bubble, NO, and a stone dropped into a vote box. Everything in the film is measured against it: who is still
allowed to say no to the leader.

- A narrator, present tense, talking to "you". On screen "you" is one kid in a loud shirt; it is your village.
- Each `[cue]` line is **one stop of the camera**: the words heard while it flies to a thing and rests on it.
  The camera never cuts: the whole film is one flight over three floating islands.
- Each `# clip` is one recording. Record it to `public/audio/what-is-a-dictator/raw/<clip>.mp3`.
- The word "dictator" is in the first line because the question asks for it (the owner's title). It is said again
  late (4.name), as the name of what the viewer has just watched.
- The last line is the subscribe line, and it is part of the explanation.
- **Nobody of modern times is named**, and no country. The one name in the film is Julius Caesar.

**Length.** 199 words in 20 lines: about 53 seconds at the pace of the reference film, which is 182 words and 49
seconds (the limits are 30–58 seconds and 110–250 words).

What each line SHOWS. Three islands: **your village** (green, bright), **the other village** (the same streets in
grey stone, under one man's banners) and, far below both, **Rome**. As in the reference film, down is earlier.

## The claims, and where each came from

An explainer that is wrong is worse than none. Every factual claim of the script is listed here. The three
Britannica pages were read in full on 2026-11-13, before the script went to the owner, and each says what this
table says it does. The Wikipedia page was read only as the excerpt a search returned; nothing in the script rests
on it alone.

| Line | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2.vote … 2.again | Where leaders are chosen by vote, a court can overrule them, people may criticise them, and they can lose the next vote. This describes "your village" in the film, the kind of place a dictatorship is the opposite of; it is not a claim about any one country | The contrast is the encyclopedia's own: a dictator rules by "the suppression of basic civil liberties" (below) |
| 4.army | Some dictators take power by force: "sometimes an army or group of army officers seized power and established a military dictatorship"; "Dictators usually resort to force or fraud to gain political power" | Britannica Kids (Students), "dictatorship": https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/dictatorship/603781 |
| 4.won | Some were elected first: "Other dictators came to power by election, after which they captured personal power by establishing one-party rule and suppressing the opposition" | The same page |
| 4.judge | Nobody can overrule him: a dictatorship is "a form of government in which one person or a small group possesses absolute power" | The same page |
| 4.jail | He punishes people who speak against him: dictators "remain in power through the use of intimidation, terror, and the suppression of basic civil liberties"; "the use of terror and propaganda to stop dissent and opposition" | The same page |
| 4.vote | Votes are ended or rigged: "force or fraud", "one-party rule and suppressing the opposition" | The same page |
| 5.word | The word comes from ancient Rome and is more than two thousand years old: "The term dictatorship comes from the Latin title dictator. In ancient Rome, a dictator was a temporary magistrate who was granted special powers to deal with state crises" | The same page |
| 5.rome | In the Roman Republic a dictator was "a temporary magistrate with extraordinary powers", "resorted to only in times of military, and later internal, crises"; the other officials "were subject to the dictator's authority". The line says "the power", not "all the power": from about 300 BC even a dictator could be appealed against | Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Roman dictator": https://www.britannica.com/topic/Roman-dictator |
| 5.six | "The dictator's term was set at six months, although he customarily laid down his powers as soon as the crisis passed" | The same page |
| 5.caesar | "Caesar acquired dictatorial powers for 10 years in 46 and for life immediately before his assassination in 44 bce". (Wikipedia, "Dictator perpetuo", dates the title between 26 January and 15 February of 44 BC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictator_for_life) | The same page |
| 5.now | Today's meaning is the one who does not give it back: "Modern dictators, however, resemble ancient tyrants rather than ancient dictators" | Britannica Kids (Students), "dictatorship" |
| 6.answer | The definition: one ruler with absolute power | The same page |

**What the film leaves out, on purpose.** It names no dictator after Caesar and no country: the encyclopedia's own
examples are Hitler, Mussolini and Stalin, and each of them is a film of its own. It does not say that Caesar was
killed weeks after he was made dictator for life, or that Rome then abolished the job. It does not tell the Romans'
favourite story, the farmer Cincinnatus who was made dictator, won his war and went back to his field: the
encyclopedia calls his story "much embellished by legend"
(https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lucius-Quinctius-Cincinnatus, read in full). It does not say that a small
group can rule the same way, or how many countries are ruled like this today: the counts differ with who is
counting. Nothing in the film contradicts any of these.

**What is put to the owner with this draft:**

1. **No modern name.** The film explains what a dictator IS, with a village, and names only Julius Caesar. Say so
   if you want famous ones named (Hitler, Stalin, Mussolini are the encyclopedia's examples). I would keep them
   out: with no names the film is true everywhere and offends nobody's country.
2. **The surprise is Rome**: "dictator" used to be a job that lasted six months and was handed back.
3. **The voice**: `voice-00`, the same narrator as every film so far.

**What has to be built for it.** The villagers, their huts, the stall, the speech bubble and the subscribe sign are
in the pack. New: a vote box and its stones, a leader's hat, a judge's desk and hammer, a statue, a balcony with
banners, soldiers, a cage, stone letters, and the Roman island (columns, steps, a purple cloak, a wreath, a row of
moons).

```narration
# clip 01-question
[1.ask]       What is a dictator?
[2.vote]      In your village, the people pick their leader with a vote.
[2.judge]     If he breaks a rule, a judge can tell him no.
[2.loud]      If he does a bad job, anyone can say so out loud.
[2.again]     And every few years they vote again, and he can lose.

# clip 02-dictator
[3.hates]     Now meet a leader who hates the word no.
[4.army]      He may march in with soldiers.
[4.won]       Or he may even win a fair vote first.
[4.judge]     Then he makes sure no judge can tell him no.
[4.jail]      He locks up people who say he is wrong.
[4.vote]      And the next vote is fixed, or it never comes.
[4.name]      That is a dictator, and no vote can make him leave.

# clip 03-rome
[5.word]      The word is over two thousand years old. It used to be a job.
[5.rome]      When ancient Rome was in danger, it gave one man the power.
[5.six]       But only for six months. Then he had to give it back.
[5.caesar]    Then Julius Caesar was made dictator for life.
[5.now]       Today the word means only that kind.
[6.answer]    So a dictator is a ruler nobody is allowed to tell no.
[7.you]       Which is why a vote matters. It is how you say no.
[7.sub]       So subscribe. Or say no. Here, you still can.
```

## Two ideas that were not written

- **The class president.** The whole thing told in one classroom: the kid who wins the vote for class president and
  then cancels every vote after it. Funny, but it makes a dictator sound like a bossy classmate.
- **A day under a dictator.** One kid's day in the grey village: what he may not say, read or ask. It shows what
  it is like, and never says what a dictator is.
