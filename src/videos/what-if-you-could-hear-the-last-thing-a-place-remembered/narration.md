## The four sentences

What if I could hear the last thing a place remembered. Every wall I touched said one sentence in somebody else's
voice — the last thing said in front of it — and then went quiet for good. I used it for a week to find out what
people really thought of me, spent every wall I liked, and then put my hand on my own bedroom wall and heard my name
in my mother's voice in a room with no windows. What it was really doing was not memory: every wall I emptied was a
sentence I had taken, and one of them was the thing my dad had been saying to me for nineteen years in a room I was
never in.

## The rule, in twelve words

**I touch a wall, and I hear the last thing it heard.**

The limit: one sentence per wall, the last one said in front of it, and it plays once — touch it again and it is silent.
The price: every wall he touches gives up its sentence. He is not listening to the past; he is spending it.

## The five jokes, before 0:20

1. `2.bench` — the bench outside the shop says "OW" in a stranger's voice, and he looks under it.
2. `2.class` — the classroom wall plays the teacher's worst joke back at him, all of it.
3. `2.bus` — the bus stop says the same sentence it has said every day for a year.
4. `2.gym` — the gym wall remembers nothing at all, and he knocks on it thinking he broke it.
5. `2.mirror` — the bathroom wall says "you look nice" in his own mother's voice.

## The misread he says out loud

`5.thought` — *"I thought walls remembered the important things."*

## What is planted and where it pays off

| Planted | Pays off |
|---|---|
| `1.once` — one sentence, then the wall goes quiet for good | `6.list` — every wall he has touched is silent, and he did it |
| `2.gym` — one wall with nothing on it | `7.wait` — it was not empty; it was the only one holding something back |
| `3.tally` — he keeps a list of the best things he heard | `6.mine` — the list has a sentence on it he did not write |
| `4.one` — a wall says his name in a room with no windows | `8.wall` — and it is the kitchen, and it was his dad |
| `3.dad` — his dad says one thing to an empty kitchen every morning | `8.said` — on the last morning his mother says it, and the wall takes it |

## The escalation, last quarter

1. `6.gone` — the walls he liked are dead, and he is the one who spent them.
2. `6.mine` — his own list has a sentence he never heard, in his own hand.
3. `8.why` — his father said it every day for nineteen years, to a room his son was never in.
