## The premise

What if I could touch any block and hear its last phrase? I used the power to find out why my best friend had gone quiet. It worked until I mistook his rehearsal for a goodbye and nearly lost him. The blocks had repeated his words, but never who he was speaking as.

**One sentence:** A boy mistakes his friend's play rehearsal for a real goodbye because blocks repeat words without context.

**The rule of the power:** Touch a block and it repeats the last phrase it heard; it cannot say who spoke, when, or why.

**The price:** I trusted scraps of overheard words more than my friend.

What is planted, and where it pays off:

| Planted | Pays off |
|---|---|
| Eli carries a script and says, “It's just a play.” | The final echoes are lines from his villain's goodbye. |
| The gym wall repeats “I can't stay.” | It is the opening line Eli rehearsed beside the stage. |
| The power gives words without a speaker or a date. | The narrator wrongly turns an old rehearsal into a message about himself. |

One teller, first person, past tense. Fast, plain, about 255 words a minute. Each cue is one shot; each clip is one voice recording.
