# That's Me. The Elbow.

A kid races Grandma's three-second timer to get more than his sleeve into her birthday photo. The selected opening is the party in motion → shutter → print. Opening on Grandma's fridge would show the evidence before the accident; opening on a stopwatch would hide who the race is for. The dishwasher outline supplied the rhythm, not the plot. No restaurant, interview, mop or copied basketball lines.

One dead-serious first-person teller; the storyteller quotes the couple's actual question. Cut to the outstretched camera and the person asking; cut back to his nod on the answer. Use the recorded breaths as action markers: button / crossing / landing. The two triples are separate repetitions. The final three are muscle memory. The old print and the new print must be pictures of **these actors, in these sets**.
