# Narration — What If You Could See How Many Times You'll Talk to Someone Again?

**Status: recorded and built to the beat sheet. One short, one story, no part two.**

Nine clips, 44 shots, ~470 words, ~112 s. Built to `Topics/MASTERPROMPT-01.md`: hook in the first second, a group
scene with a number over every head, a plant he waves away, a rule he misreads, a rule he spends, the reveal that
re-reads the plant, and a CTA after the payoff — never inside a scene.

**Premise, four sentences.** What if every person's head carried a number: the conversations you still have left with
them. I used it to check my dad, who had four. I spent two of them on arguments about a Sunday he was not coming to.
What the number was really counting was how many times I would choose to speak first.

**The rule.** A number over every head: the conversations left between you and them. **It only goes down when you are
not looking at it, and it never shows your own.**

| Planted | Pays off |
|---|---|
| His dad: 4 (2.dad) | Those four were four chances to say the true thing, and he wastes two (8.win) |
| The cook: 1, and she is kind anyway (4.cook) | A number of 1 is not a warning: it is one conversation worth having (13.list) |
| The friend's number is huge, and he never checks it (6.friend) | People you never count are the ones who stay (13.re) |
| He never sees his own number (3.limit) | Because his own is not his to spend: it is theirs (13.mine) |
| "It goes down when you are not looking" (3.rule) | He stops looking at his dad to protect the 4, and it drops to 1 (10.turn) |
