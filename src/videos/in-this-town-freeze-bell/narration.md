# In This Town, We Had to Freeze Whenever the Bell Rang

**Script approved for production. The owner requested the MP4 and selected the English narrator.**

**Format:** the full-length InThisTown story. One kid, first person, past tense. The timeline comes from the approved recordings, not hand-set shot lengths.

**Measured production checks:** 697 spoken words · 18 beats · 82 shots · 9 aligned recordings of the selected narrator, `voice-00`. Runtime **165.1333 seconds** at **254 WPM**. **Premise:** A kid follows the rope of a bell that makes his whole town freeze. Outside the town wall, he discovers that their home is a model inside a giant child's toy box. Staying still has kept them from being collected—and his missing neighbor is still alive under a jar.

Each `[cue]` line below is one planned shot. The nine `# clip` headings are recording groups, not scene boundaries. Beat numbers run from 1 to 18. Only the words after a cue are spoken; the notes below the script are not narration.

## The clues and their answers

| Planted | Paid back |
|---|---|

| Scraping before the bell (`2.scrape`, `6.scrape`, `14.scrape`). | The stiff lid scrapes against the case rim before its linkage trips the bell. Show the same sound and mechanical movement at `17.rope`, not a new unexplained noise. |
| A rope that twitches by itself and vanishes through a wall (`6.twitch`–`6.wall`). | It runs behind the model town's scenery to the lid (`15.rope`, `17.rope`). The boy has found the actual route, not a magic passage. |
| Heads down, shadows, and two rings (`3.lookdown`–`3.second`, `6.shadow`, `7.rule`). | The shadows belong to the child handling the case. The first ring warns that the lid is opening; the second means it is closing. The final shadow belongs to the same hand. |
| The neighbor moves too soon and disappears (`2.neighbor`–`2.empty`). | He is alive under a glass jar beside the case (`17.jar`–`17.alive`). The child collects the figures that move. |
| “What did we look like when none of us moved?” (`8.awake`). | “We had been pretending to be toys” (`17.toys`). This answers the hook rather than adding a separate monster. |
| The neighbor's bun and empty stool (`3.bun`, `12.pack`, `14.empty`). | The extra bun is for him (`17.bun`). The kid reaches to help, and that movement draws the child's attention. |

## Picture and continuity notes for the eventual build

The owner has approved the script and authorized production. These notes remain the continuity contract for the shots.

- **Three places:** a warm, busy miniature village; the cold, narrow space behind its scenery; the dim giant bedroom outside the case. The town uses the approved InThisTown pack. The reveal changes our understanding of its scale, not the villagers' design.
- **A deliberate freeze, not stopped time:** people hold their poses. Loose objects still fall; dust, shadows, cloth and the camera keep moving. Baskets are held, never suspended in midair. The baker's low piles and school exercises make the habit visible before it becomes frightening.
- **The hook:** start on the teller's face with the market stacked behind him. Freeze the people on “bell,” not the whole image. Do not show the giant, the case rim or a readable hand-shaped shadow yet.
- **The cast:** one teller and one narrator voice. The friend, neighbor and elder are distinguished by outfit and role, not names. Give the neighbor one unmistakable approved outfit so he is recognizable under glass; preserve it in `2.neighbor`, the memory in `3.bun`, and `17.jar`. Use the pack's Player looks where a performance requires moving hands; never unfold or redesign the pack's Villager arms.
- **The neighbor's memory:** `3.bun` is a short, clearly motivated recollection of sharing food on the stool near the market, not his return to the present-day town. His house and stool stay empty until the reveal.
- **The route is physical:** the bell rope passes through the wall to a linkage behind the scenery. The loose-board gap is a separate, adjacent opening. The hay-bale lookout fails before a bell, so the joke does not contradict the danger. The kid tests the gap after dark, then returns to watch the mechanism in use.
- **The packing shot:** overhead and moving. Water, torch, rope and bread each enter on their spoken noun. Use the torch to inspect the dark gap; put it out when the strip of outside light appears. His own rope helps him reach the hinge screw at `16.look` without yanking the bell rope.
- **The edge:** repeat the school, bread piles and stool from the tour, with the same layout. Bag tightened, knees bent, breath taken: three small actions before the scrape. Keep the teller visible at least every third shot throughout, including noun inserts with his hands or face at the edge.
- **The viewpoint conceals the truth fairly:** at street level, the model town's tall scenery blocks the room beyond it. The inhabitants are trained to keep their heads down. The outside shelf and hinge screw are glimpsed at his scale before the reveal establishes their real size. Do not show an unrelated outdoor horizon behind the case.
- **The reveal is a picture first:** `17.box` pulls back to show the entire town inside a glass-lidded wooden display box in a child's bedroom. `17.boy` supplies the towering child. No explanatory sign, overlay or second narrator is needed.
- **The bell mechanism:** one brief strike when the lid begins opening and one as it closes, not constant ringing while the rope is taut. `17.rope` revisits the opening already heard in `15.ring`, now from the revealing angle; `17.second` advances to the present closure. Make that replay legible with the same rope jerk and chime. No unexplained third ring.
- **The last danger has a cause:** the jar is on the same shelf, close to the kid's exit, so reaching it needs no teleport or new journey. It is an inverted glass jar; he reaches toward its lower edge. The second bell makes the town safe inside the closed case, not the kid outside it. His movement toward his neighbor catches the child's eye. End before the hand reaches him; no injury or extra twist.
- **Sound motif:** the same scrape and bell first interrupt a playful town rhythm, then reveal the mechanism. Keep the lid scrape, chime and moving shadow synchronized to their picture. The final shadow repeats the earlier cue without a new monster roar. A full music/effects sheet comes after approved voice and pictures, not at this script stage.
