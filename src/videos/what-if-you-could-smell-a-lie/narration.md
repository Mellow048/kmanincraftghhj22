# Narration — What If You Could Smell a Lie?

**Status: DRAFT 1 for the owner. One short, one story, no part two.** 56 shots, ~470 words, ~110 s at 255 words a
minute, median shot ~1.9 s. Built to the topic's fourteen beats (`Topics/WhatIf/SCRIPT.md`) and to the engine of
Monster Village: a rule the hero misreads, a plant he waves away, a list, a re-read.

**The premise, in four sentences.** What if every lie I heard smelled of burnt toast. I used it to find out who was
lying to me, and then to be the kid nobody could lie to. It worked until the day it turned up in the one person who
never lied. What it was really doing was telling me who was being kind.

**The rule, in one line.** I smell a lie the moment it is said — **and I can never smell my own.**

**The price.** Every lie he exposes, he exposes in front of somebody, so the power makes him right and alone at the
same rate.

| Planted | Pays off |
|---|---|
| "My mum has never smelled of toast. Not once." (6.never) | She never lied: she said almost nothing, and "I'm fine" was the whole of it (13.mum) |
| He tells the friend his mum is the only honest person in town (6.honest) | The friend says nothing, and the nothing has no smell: silence is not honesty (6.silent) |
| The friend's line "it tells you it is a lie, not why" (9.why) | Every lie in act four was a kindness he could not smell the reason for (13.back) |
| "He is coming Sunday" — toast, and he is pleased to have caught her (7.caught) | The cards in act four are all about the Sunday that is not happening (12.card) |
| The cake, the cards, the candles, every smile (10.cake) | They were all lying about the same thing, to the same boy (13.all) |
