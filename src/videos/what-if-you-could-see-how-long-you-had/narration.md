**The premise, in four sentences.** What if I could see how long I had? Not how long I had to live — how long I had
before I lost my temper. I used it to get through a week without getting hit, and it made me very good at school and
very bad at people. What it was really doing was telling me why people go off, and the only clocks that were not
counting belonged to the people who had somebody to talk to.

**One sentence:** A boy who can see how close everybody is to losing their temper spends a week managing other people
and never once has a conversation, then finds his own clock counting down and learns the power was never about time.

**The rule of the power, in one line a viewer could repeat:** A clock floats over somebody's head and counts down to
the moment they snap. **It never says what will set them off** — only how long is left. He cannot start one, stop
one, or add a second to one.

**The price.** Reading a clock is not free: while he is reading, he is not talking. It costs him the week, and then it
costs him Jo.

What is planted, and where it pays off:

| Planted | Pays off |
|---|---|
| "A clock told you when. Nothing told you why." (3.limit) | "It was never counting down. It was counting on me." (13.on) |
| Jo has no clock at all (5.jo) | Her clock appears at one the second he reaches her (12.one) |
| "The school has never been this quiet" (8.quiet) — reads as him being good at it | It is quiet because his own clock is the only one counting (10.mine) |
| "Every Friday, two people" (4.friday) — a clock he gets wrong | The clocks were not wrong; he had the reason wrong (13.why) |
| "Too easy" (3.easy) | "It was easy because I was not in it" (13.me) |

**Sentences of six words or fewer** (they carry the plot alone): Then his shoulders went up. · Nothing told you why. ·
Jo does not have a clock. · Then I looked up. · It said ten. · It said one. · It was counting on me. · Her clock went
out. · Every clock turned round.
