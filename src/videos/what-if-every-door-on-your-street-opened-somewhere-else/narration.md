## The four sentences

What if every door on my street opened somewhere else. I learned the whole map by heart and used it to go anywhere
I wanted without being seen. It worked, until I opened a door and found a room with my own handwriting on the wall.
What it was really doing was taking me somewhere I had already been: every shortcut I took was a door somebody else
had to walk through the long way.

## The rule, in twelve words

**The door you pick decides where you come out.**

The limit: it works on doors on his street, and only while the street is dark. In daylight every door is just a door.

## The price

Every shortcut costs somebody else something. Every door he goes through, his mother walks round the long way: she is
not locked out of the map, she is paying for it on foot. He skips a queue and the queue does not move for him: one
woman is at the front of it, letting everybody past, at nine at night and still there at six the next evening.
He never sees the cost because he is never in the room when it lands.

## The five jokes, before 0:20

1. `2.fridge` — he opens the fridge to get breakfast out of it, and walks out of it into school.
2. `2.neighbour` — the neighbour steps out of his own wardrobe into the street, in a dressing gown, furious.
3. `2.pool` — a dare: open the blue door at number 9 and come out in the swimming pool.
4. `2.dad` — his dad opens the back door, walks into a wall, and tries again.
5. `2.pizza` — a pizza arrives twenty minutes late through a door it was not ordered from.

## The misread he says out loud

`6.only` — *"I thought I was the only one who knew which door was which."*

## The escalation, last quarter

1. `8.higher` — the writing on the wall is his own, but older, and higher up than he can reach.
2. `11.room` — the room is not a stranger's: it is his own bedroom, seen from the outside.
3. `11.count` — the tally has been kept by somebody else, and it outnumbers his.
4. `12.paid` — his mum has been paying for every shortcut, in walking, all month.
