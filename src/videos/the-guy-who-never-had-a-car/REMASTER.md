# Remaster — The Guy Who Never Had a Car

## Owner's review driving this revision
The opening works. The rest felt slow-motion, too static, the Almost shout was not fast-forwarded,
and subtitles drifted badly. This revision keeps the hook and approved wording, not the dead holds.
No new narrator: voice-20 is retained. No changes to the shared engine or other approved films.

## Edit changes
- **108.0 → 59.9 seconds** (1,797 frames at 30 fps).
- **52 shots**, with cuts driven by measured word onsets. The opening remains 6.8 seconds;
  post-hook shots are mostly brief action/reaction beats rather than silent portraits.
- **AHH / ALMOST / ALMOST: 2.4×**, including the recorded pauses, with pitch preserved.
- **HUS: 1.65×**. The original opening audio is retained in `hook.wav`.
- Other takes use a modest per-take tempo change (1.1–1.25×); see `remaster-edit.json`.
- Fresh 158 BPM original phonk-style bed after the hook, shorter effects and a half-second pigeon pause.
- No new material is generated by the rejected standalone renderer.

## Real actor/prop movement
- Rapid, larger scrubbing strokes, alternating arms, torso rotation and water trajectories.
- A rig-attached toothbrush with a crouched actor; the bucket no longer blocks the contact shot.
- Fast recoil/arm reactions during the Almost shout, not a motionless face close-up.
- Hands cross and pull during the glove callback; no unattached floating glove prop.
- Moving cars and matching tracking cameras; travel is in the direction the cars face.
- Seated driver poses, head turns and quicker friends' recognition gestures.
- A tossed/growing sponge pile, flipping calendar, cloth wipes and a short eating gesture.
- Actual forward walks into/away through the dealership, rather than backward sliding.
- Dirt stays on the wreck through the final training montage until the wipe removes it.
- Film-local title mesh depth settings keep the meme cards readable over foreground action.

## Caption rebuilding
`alignment.json` contains forced alignment on the clean voice-20 takes using the repository's
`narration.py` PocketSphinx aligner. There is **no equal-duration word-spacing fallback** in this edit.
The repeated HUS syllables confused speech alignment, so their boundaries were measured as acoustic
bursts on the original recording instead. Nine bursts are audible in that take, despite the original
TTS prompt asking for eight; the measured caption count follows the recording.

Each word interval is transformed through the actual measured tempo-filter output length, placed at
its real mix start, and given a small 25 ms anticipation where possible. Cuts are rounded to the
30 fps grid. `timing.json` and the engine's own caption renderer are used for the final video.

`check-car-remaster.py` matches each processed dry take back against the encoded mix by waveform
correlation. The first run measured take placement errors between -8.3 and +0.3 ms, under one frame.
This tests the mix clock, **not** the linguistic accuracy of every forced-aligned phoneme.
The same checker can test the rendered MP4 for mux offset at four different points.

## Reproduction
Install `numpy`, `imageio-ffmpeg` and `pocketsphinx` in the Python environment.
The chosen raw takes are now committed under `public/audio/the-guy-who-never-had-a-car/raw/`.

```bash
python3 scripts/remaster-car-reel.py
python3 scripts/check-car-remaster.py
npm run typecheck
npm run check
```

`remaster-car-reel.py` builds the audio mix, timed script, caption track, literal `storyScene` directions,
and `remaster-edit.json`. It reads the measured alignment data instead of guessing word timings.
The video is rendered with the ordinary repository GitHub Actions/Remotion framework workflow.

Procedural Minecraft rigs and models remain the visual style. Faster cuts and extra motion are creative
responses to the owner's retention feedback, not evidence of improved audience retention analytics.
