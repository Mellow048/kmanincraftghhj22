## Stage 6 — story and picture

Read against [the owner's master prompt](../../../Topics/InThisTown/MASTERPROMPT.md), not against an assumed retention target. I used three passes of contact frames, then stepped through 141 frames of the locked picture without captions or sound at the payment, treasure, stripe, lift and two-fish beats. I also inspected 83 frames decoded from the verified MP4, including a frame from every shot and frames immediately before and after key contacts. These are frame and signal checks, not a continuous watch or listen.

**Sound verification.** Six measured Finn recordings, tempo ×1.2035; rerunning the aligned build with 80 ms clip gaps reproduced the timing, narration, mix and lock byte for byte. The owner's background track is 19.2 dB below speech on average; 10 beds, 87 effects (69.7/min), none buried by the topic test. Speech margin is 15.6 dB median and 4.8 dB worst (the dive). Source mix: −14.8 LUFS, −2.5 dBTP. Checked the timings and levels; did not listen to this as a person on a phone would.

**What was learned, not copied.** The reviewed 175-second *Monster Village Part 1* opens on a composition that gains information as the characters arrive, resolves a wrong interpretation in the middle, and uses a gesture again at the payoff. Here one payment gives the action inside the first shot, the matching stripe reverses “the dolphin only found gold,” and the count changes from one fish to two. The owner’s benchmark is a reference, not a distribution prediction. If Studio data is available later, check initial stayed-to-watch, then check the engaged curve around the dive and stripe separately; no claim about a specific historical drop.

**Remaining weaknesses.** Some physical acting is intentionally broad and block-rigged: the swim is stiffer than the dock poses and the forearm grip reads by silhouette rather than fingers. The chest is briefly crossed by the swimmer before the tighter gold shot, and the rescue carries several fast lines over a small area of the set. On a phone, the shots to watch most closely are the red tip at 0:30 and the lever at 0:53. Tech and frame checks cannot establish how the narration plays to an audience, or guarantee retention.
