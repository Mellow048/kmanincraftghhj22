family and the origin are the *rest of beat 1*, not the hook.

| **this film, draft 2** | seven lines, family and the door folded in | **57** | **14.6 s** | — too slow: a whole beat standing where the hook goes |

The three-line shape is always the same:

1. **Where I am** (bottom of the mountain / only cat / only human).
2. **What everybody else is** (the rich, up there / creepers / monsters).
3. **What I want, said flat.** In the other agent's film it is in the past — "One day, we were going to join them" —
   so it sounds like it already failed. The owner's change for this film (2026-11-25) is to say the want plainly
   instead: **"I want to sleep on it."** Present tense, three seconds after the bed is named. The doom is carried
   by what a bed does in the Nether, not by the grammar.

### Three promises, made early, paid late

| Promised | At | Paid | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I want to sleep on it" | **0:05** | **0:50** | He carries the cellar bed into the square and it takes the roof off the shed in front of four hundred people |
| "She pointed at the floor" | 0:12 | **1:33** | The black stone frame with gold in it, then lit — the door home |
| "One night's sleep" | 0:29 | **1:43** | A village asleep on the other side; then four hundred people on their backs in a red street |

The first is the one that holds the middle. From second six the viewer knows two things the teller does not: what a
bed does here, and that he is going to lie on one. **He spends forty seconds walking towards it**, gets it wrong
first with a fake bed that does not explode, and concludes beds are safe. That is the Creeper Parents trick — the
answer he cannot see — and it is why the middle cannot sag.

### The rest of the retention

- **0:15–0:30** the world, then the real want — "one night's sleep" — which is the hook's want, grown up. Nothing
  else is asked for in the film.
- **0:30–0:43** three tries, each shorter and funnier than the last: the square, the strider, the fake bed. The
  false victory lands on a joke ("Nothing happened. So I thought beds were safe.").
- **0:43–0:56** the promise paid, and it costs him the shed, the soup and a month in the heat — which is what puts
  him where the door is. The explosion is a price, not a climax; the film does not stop for it.
- **1:06–1:15** the list said back, then a man sitting on the floor: the first person in the film who stops
  voluntarily, and the first hint that the rule has an author.
- **1:15–1:29** the reveal: 11, 10, 12, 9, then the line that turns line one inside out.
- **1:29–1:57** the want in a picture, and an ending that is a gesture, not a speech.

## Minecraft facts this stands on

Beds explode in the Nether. The Nether has no day, no night and no sun. Water evaporates the moment it is placed
there. There are no sheep in the Nether, so there is no wool to make a bed from. Glowstone is the light. Striders
walk on lava. A ruined portal is a frame of black stone with gold in it, standing in the world and unlit. Mushrooms
and fungi are the food.

Looked up before a line is recorded — three are said out loud, and I cannot reach the wiki from here:
1. the size of a bed's explosion (the shed, not the town);
2. that a ruined portal lights with flint and steel like any other frame;
3. that there are no sheep anywhere in the Nether.
