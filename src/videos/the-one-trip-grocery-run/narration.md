# The One-Trip Grocery Run — narration and locked script

**Owner-approved to write and produce without another script review (2026-11-18).** The owner approved the concept and said he would not review the script. This version is in production. Keep the approved Elbow reference exactly as-is; the new film has no ear prop, photo gag or ear joke.

Topic: `recreation`. Real-life grocery carrying, played straight by block people; no game mechanics. The named skill is carrying every bag in one load. The chant is attached only to visible attempts at that skill.

**One sentence:** A kid trains to carry all the groceries in one trip, gets to help a neighbor, then discovers the lone potato he left in the trunk.

**Who speaks:** one deadpan first-person narrator, also the kid. His sister and neighbor speak through action and faces; no extra spoken voices needed.

**Hook alternatives (selected A):**
- **A — selected:** At frame one, a kid completes an overloaded grocery run; one potato is visible in the trunk behind him. The chant lands on each carrying move. “One trip. Every bag. Even the eggs.” It invites the question, “How did he do it?” without delaying the skill.
- **B:** A dozen bags swing around a kid in a doorway; an egg carton opens in his hands. “The door opened. The eggs did too.” Funny, but starts with failure instead of the skill's surprising result.
- **C:** A tiny potato sits alone in a huge empty trunk while the kid turns back. “I almost did it in one trip.” Strong payoff, but gives away the last joke in the opening.

**Plant and payoff:**

| Planted | Pays off |
|---|---|
| The picture and words establish one trip as his ridiculous rule. | The neighbor's bags become a useful, real test, not an unrelated party rescue. |
| The eggs are precarious during the failed doorway rep. | The carton opens, and a shell rolls out of his shoe the next morning. |
| A potato is plainly visible at the edge of the trunk in the opening; the kid's overloaded arms block his view. | His sister lifts the blanket on the apparently empty trunk. He makes one extra trip for one potato. |

**Direction:** 7 beats, about 75–85 seconds after aligned voice and physical pauses. Every visible full-load carry gets its own HUS triple; setup, balancing, weighing and reactions are not falsely chanted. Large on-set pixel sign `ONE TRIP`; captions match speech. Keep the tiny potato in frame in the opening and repeat the trunk angle for the payoff.
