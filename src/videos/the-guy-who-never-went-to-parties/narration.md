# Narration — The Guy Who Never Went To Parties

**Status: DRAFT for the owner.** Built to `Topics/InThisTown` (short cut, 14 beats).

Topic: `Topics/InThisTown`. Read `RETENTION.md`: every scene is an argument with the viewer, not a report.

## What the story is

A kid in a town where **everybody parties every night and nobody has ever eaten a cooked meal**. He wants to be a
cook. It turns out he is the only person in town who has ever wanted anything you can eat.

## The premise, in four sentences

In this town, every single night, the whole town goes to a party. Nobody goes home, and nobody eats dinner. I wanted
to be a cook, so while they danced I stayed in and chopped. It turned out they were not lazy — nobody in that town
had ever eaten cooked food, so nobody had ever thought to want it.

**One sentence:** the only kid in a town that never eats dinner decides to become a chef.

## The open question (this is what holds a viewer)

**Planted at 0:08, named at 0:22, answered at 0:36:** *why does nobody in this town cook?*

The answer is the engine of the film: **they have never tasted cooked food.** Not once, not one of them. You cannot
want a thing you have never had. Every joke in the first third is a clue to it, and every clue is a joke.

## The five jokes, before 0:20

1. `2.cereal` — his mum serves cereal at eleven at night and calls it dinner.
2. `2.fridge` — the fridge contains exactly one thing, and that thing is a fridge.
3. `2.onion` — a kid at a party eats a raw onion like an apple, with both hands.
4. `2.dog` — the dog eats better than the children; the dog eats grass.
5. `2.vending` — the town's only restaurant is a vending machine, and there is a queue for it.

## The memes it is built on (researched, not guessed)

| The meme | Where it is | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| **"HUS HUS HUS"** | Hook (0:03) and the whole training arc | Not a chopping sound. It is the **running/training chant** from Daniel LaBelle run edits, Forrest Gump and the Spider-Man run memes: rhythmic breathing at speed. So he chants it *while training*, treating cooking like athletics. That is the joke, and it is why it lands twice |
| **The egg flip fail** | `4.egg`–`4.almost` | The real cooking-fail format ("an almost perfect egg flip", "moments before disaster"). The word is **"almost"**, said alone, to nobody |
| **"bro come on / you only live once"** | `2.bro`, `8.how` | The friend-who-spams-invites format. Used straight, then turned over at the reunion |
| **"In this town, …"** | The opening | The Roblox/Cluewick story short format: one impossible rule, stated flat, then the consequences |
| **"Training."** | 1:33 | The deadpan one-word answer. One word, pause, then the comic payoff |

## The voice

Male, mid, dramatic, and completely straight — the narrator never admits the joke. Every absurd line is delivered as
a fact. The comedy is that he is *serious about cooking* and the town is serious about *partying*, and both of them
are treated as grave matters.

## Rules it is built to

- One teller, first person, past tense. About 238 words a minute, with a third of a second of air
  before each new step of the story (`OWNER.md`, the pace he approved on the iron golem film).
  254 words a minute with no gaps was part of why this film's first render was rejected.
- Each `[cue]` line is **one shot**. Cut on the noun. 5–12 words.
- First line starts "In this town". Last line is the subscribe line.
- Short cut: 14 beats, 250–520 words, one to two minutes.
- Somebody else speaks about every fifteen seconds, and the teller is in his own story in every line.
