# Narration — What If You Could Hear Every Unsaid Thing?

**Status: scripted and recorded. One short, one story.**

Nine clips, ~40 shots, ~400 words, ~95 s. Written to `Topics/MASTERPROMPT-01.md`: the impossible fact in the
first second, the rule stated plainly before it is used, escalation through the people around him, a low point,
the title turned upside down at the end, and the ask after the payoff — never inside a scene.

**Premise, three sentences.** From the age of nine I can hear every sentence a person decides not to say, but only
if it was meant for me. I used it to read my friends and my teachers, and it cost me the friend who moved away.
At the end I stood close enough to hear my father's four words properly, and they were not what I had assumed.

**The rule.** You hear the sentence at the moment someone decides not to say it, in their own voice, and only you
hear it. It fades in seconds. It never tells you why they held it back.

| Planted | Pays off |
|---|---|
| "Same four words, every night, six years" (5.words) | The four words are not praise, they are a question (8.real) |
| He assumes what the four words are (5.assume) | He is wrong, and the film has never let us hear them (8.four) |
| The friend's unsaid thing, heard too late (7.late) | Being able to hear is not the same as being able to answer (9.say) |
| "I learned to stay quiet" (6.quiet) | His answer has to be said out loud, and loudly, to work (9.say) |

```narration
# clip 01-hook
[1.hook]     What if I could hear every sentence you decided not to say?
[1.mouth]    My dad has said the same thing to me every night for six years. He has never opened his mouth.
[1.rule]     Since I was nine, I hear every sentence a person decides not to say.
[1.only]     Only the person it was meant for can hear it.

# clip 02-school
[2.first]    The first one was my teacher.
[2.maths]    She looked at my test and decided not to say it.
[2.heard]    I heard it anyway. "This kid is going to fail."
[2.said]     I said nothing. I was nine.

# clip 03-friend
[3.friend]   My best friend almost said something in the hall.
[3.almost]   "Don't sit with me today."
[3.heard]    I heard it before he had finished deciding.
[3.why]      He never said it. So he thought I already knew.

# clip 04-mum
[4.mum]      My mum does it in the kitchen.
[4.money]    Every month she decides not to say, "we can't afford it."
[4.smile]    Then she smiles and says the food is ready.
[4.keep]     I never told her I heard.

# clip 05-dad
[5.dad]      My dad's one is quieter than hers.
[5.door]     Every night he stands in my doorway and decides not to say it.
[5.words]    The same four words. Every night. Six years.
[5.assume]   I always assumed I knew what they were.

# clip 06-use
[6.use]      So I learned to stay quiet.
[6.quiet]    When I say nothing, other people start deciding.
[6.hunt]     I stood in the corridor and collected them like coins.
[6.cheap]    It felt like getting everyone's secrets for free.

# clip 07-cost
[7.cost]     It isn't free. You hear what people almost think of you.
[7.weird]    "He's weird." "He's not the same since his dad got quiet."
[7.move]     And the day my best friend moved, I heard his.
[7.late]     "I should have sat with him." Four hundred miles away, too late.

# clip 08-turn
[8.turn]     Last month I stood closer than usual.
[8.close]    Close enough to hear all of it, not just the first word.
[8.four]     The four words were not "I'm proud of you."
[8.real]     They were, "do you still need me?"

# clip 09-end
[9.night]    He stood in my door and decided not to say it again.
[9.say]      So I said it first. Out loud. "I still need you."
[9.after]    And the sentence he had carried for six years was never decided again.
[9.sub]      Subscribe if you would answer out loud.
```
