## Narration

### 1. The secret

In this town, I was the only one who could stop time for five seconds. Nobody at school knew. Not even my best friend.

My mother was a villager. I wasn't. She'd adopted me before I could remember anything. Nobody would tell me where she'd found me. Whenever I asked her, she changed the subject. But she always closed the curtains first.

### 2. School and the first stop

By my teens, I'd learned not to ask at breakfast. Our classroom had villagers, players, and a teacher who threw chalk. My best friend saved me the seat beside his. He split his bread with me every lunch. Before school, we fed my neighbor's rabbit. One black ear. It always bit my shoelaces.

I'd found my power by dropping a glass. I wanted it to stop. It did. So did the water, the clock, and my mother. I counted five seconds. Then the glass smashed.

### 3. The lesson and the warning

During a stop, I could move whatever I touched. But when I let go, it waited for time. I once stopped the teacher's chalk right by my nose. I was so proud I forgot to move. Five seconds later, it hit me anyway.

I told my mother about the glass. She put down the bowl she was washing. "Don't let anyone see you do that." Who was she hiding me from? She checked the lock instead of answering.

### 4. The locker

One morning, two boys shoved my friend into a locker. His book hit the floor. Nobody helped. They swung the door straight at his nose. I stopped it with an inch left.

I pulled him clear, put his book back in his arms, and stepped back beside him. Time returned. They slammed an empty locker. My friend thought I'd yanked him out quickly. I let him think that.

At lunch, he pushed his whole loaf toward me. He said he wished he could protect me too. Then he explained the math until I finally got it. When the teacher called on me, I had the answer.

### 5. The night rescue

Then one night, I heard shouting outside. My old neighbor had fallen outside his door. Two zombies were coming through his gate. Behind them, a skeleton raised its bow. The arrow left before I reached him.

I stopped time. The arrow hung over his chest. I pulled him over the doorstep, and kicked the door shut behind us. Time returned. The arrow stuck in the wood. Outside, the zombies scratched. Inside, he was still breathing.

### 6. The man at the window

He thanked me and handed me a carrot. "For the rabbit. He'll bite if you forget." At home, my mother checked me for scratches. I said the golem had dealt with the mobs. She shut my window before she went to bed.

Later, a crunch outside woke me. Through the window, I saw a man dressed in white. He had a beard, and no lantern. He knelt beside my neighbor's rabbit. Ice crawled from his fingers around its legs. He picked it up. "Finally. Something to eat."

### 7. The chase

I saw the black ear and the little nose twitching. I ran outside. "That's not yours!" He looked straight at me. Then he tucked the rabbit under his arm and ran. I chased him past the neighbor's gate. If I stopped him, he'd see what I could do. But I couldn't leave the rabbit.

He threw out his free hand. Three blocks of ice shot down the path at me. The first smashed a hole in a stone wall. The second was already in front of my face. I remembered the chalk.

### 8. Five seconds

I stopped time and grabbed a rock. Ran in front of him. Set it in his path. Then stepped aside.

Time came back. The ice smashed where I'd just been. His foot caught the rock. He stumbled, spun around, and landed on his back. I lifted the rabbit out of his arm. Its nose was still twitching.

### 9. The prince

He rubbed his head, then slowly got onto one knee. My mother was watching from our doorway. "A time stop ability..." he whispered. "I finally found you." "The prince of our village."
