## The proposal

**One sentence:** A teenager hides his five-second time stop while protecting his villager friend and an old neighbor, until saving that neighbor's rabbit exposes his power to an ice-wielding stranger who recognizes him as a missing prince.

**Four sentences:** In this town, I alone could stop time for five seconds. My adoptive villager mother refused to say where she had found me and warned me never to show anyone my power. I used it secretly to help my friend and my neighbor, then openly when an ice-powered rabbit thief attacked me. The man I knocked down knelt before me and called me the prince of his village.

### Cast and continuity — part of this proposal

- The **same hero as Freeze Bell**, now visibly a teenager and wearing a **red tee**, with his existing skin `#dba47c`, hair `#3b2319`, blue pants `#314b79`, and bare forearms. Do not recolor or modify the finished Freeze Bell film. Proposed teen scale: `0.95`, rather than that film's `0.8`; confirm framing against the actual model geometry.
- A Minecraft school with **villager and player-model classmates** from the existing packs. His best friend is a **villager**, not another player. He helps the hero with schoolwork, so their friendship is reciprocal.
- His adoptive mother is a **villager**. In this draft she knows about the power; his friend, classmates and neighbors do not.
- The old villager he rescues is also the rabbit's owner. The rabbit's **one black ear** makes its identity readable before the hero leaves his window.
- The ice man is a **bearded player-model adult in white clothing**, not a villager. Reuse the pack's bearded player with clothing overrides.

### Power and ending — approved with Draft 2

- The stop lasts **five seconds**. **No cooldown, exhaustion cost, extra ability, or longer stop is added.**
- Proposed staging convention: he can move people and objects he touches during a stop; once released, an object waits for time to resume. The approved counter places the touched rock in the runner's path; it stays put after release. The owner approved this convention with the script.
- The climax covers the **whole requested confrontation**, not only the rabbit theft: ice projectiles at full speed, stop, pick up rock, move in front, set the rock in his path, step aside, resume, foot contact, stumble/spin/back landing, and recognition.
- Proposed final spoken line: **“The prince of our village.”** End there, with a brief reaction/black. **No subscribe line and no promise of a second part.** The owner approved this departure for this film alone; implement its own scoped ending exception, without generalizing Freeze Bell's exception.

| The fallen glass establishes the five-second stop | Every rescue and the fight obey the same duration |

## Approved choreography and production constraints

These remain the production constraints. See the production QA record for which checks have actually run.

- Built from the InThisTown starter after explicit approval. The current starter defaults to the short cut; explicitly use the full-film profile for this script. Borrow the existing school geometry without changing the topic.
- Time stopping is not blue ice: stopped people, particles, shadows of moving things and projectiles all share a frozen world clock, while the hero and camera continue. The man's blue frost is a separate, physical effect.
- Every shown complete stop must be **150 frames at 30 fps**, measured from the stop to world motion resuming. Do not stretch five seconds into a long action montage. The measured word timing now drives all seven 150-frame intervals, with frame-quantized global clocks across cuts. A hook or memory insert may excerpt a stop, but must not imply a longer one.
- Locker rescue: hero begins within arm's reach; friend moves less than a metre; book lies at their feet. Keep both boys facing the locker, so the secret remains plausible.
- Night rescue: old villager starts on his own doorstep, not far down a lane. The open door is within reach; hero moves him roughly a metre and shuts it within the stop. Keep the arrow's trajectory continuous across resumption. The old man is flinching with his eyes shut when the arrow is loosed, so he experiences being helped, not a witnessed demonstration of the power.
- Fight: stage the two runners only about **two metres apart** at the stop; show the loose rock at the hero's feet before he takes it. Use one visible route around the suspended ice to reach the man's front and a short sidestep afterward. The rock is placed on the path; the next foot catches it after time resumes. No teleport or second power.
- The rabbit's limbs are ice-bound but its face remains visible and its nose moves before/after the fight. It is alive when rescued. No cooking or graphic injury is shown.
- Do not add a magical thaw or an offscreen rabbit outcome. The immediate outcome here is recovery from the man's arm; the last beat is his recognition.
- Apply Part 2's **wind-up → action → impact → recovery** staging. Show the hero bend and take the rock, his continuous run around the suspended ice, the lowering hand's contact and release, and the sidestep. Show the man's stumble and fall rather than cutting directly to him on the ground. Keep the rabbit visible and safely supported through that fall.

Speed comes from acceleration, scenery passing, and the impact/recovery, not simply more cuts or a camera shake. During a stop, all background particles and character animation clocks must actually hold.
