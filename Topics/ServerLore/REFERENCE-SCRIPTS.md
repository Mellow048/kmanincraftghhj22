# Server Lore Reference Scripts & Plain-English Voice Study

Study both viral 50k+ references below before writing a Server Lore script. Notice **how simple the English is**: a 9-year-old listening on a phone understands every single word on the first listen.

---

## 1. The Plain-English Formula (Zero "Wise Old Man" / Zero AI Analogies)

Both viral videos hit the **exact same numbers**: **`~250–260 words`**, **`~88 seconds`**, **`8–12 words per sentence`**, **Grade 3–4 vocabulary**.

### What makes the narration work
1. **Start sentences with `So`, `But`, `And`, `Because`** — this creates forward momentum like a friend telling you a crazy story at lunch. Never use formal essay transitions (*"However"*, *"Consequently"*, *"Unbeknownst to the community"*).
2. **State facts directly, never poetically:**
   * **Good (9-yr-old):** `"But she wasn't a griefer. She was Leo's mom."`
   * **Good (9-yr-old):** `"But Eli wasn't bad at the game. He was born deaf."`
   * **Bad (wise old man / AI):** `"Beneath the cruel mockery of the server lay a quiet truth nobody dared to see."`
3. **One concrete visual per sentence:** Every line names a thing the camera can literally show (`3:00 a.m.`, `empty chest`, `threw the book in lava`, `47 times`, `scoreboard at spawn`, `wired to lamps on every wall`).
4. **Use short 4–7 word gut punches at emotional peaks:**
   * `"The whole chat went quiet."`
   * `"And he didn't."`
   * `"Here, he was just a player."`
   * `"He had set an alarm."`
5. **Explain the Minecraft mechanic in 2 plain sentences max:**
   * `"Because one block in Minecraft can hear for you, the sculk sensor. When something moves near it, it sends a redstone signal."`
6. **End with a 1-sentence story-locked Subscribe line:**
   * `"So subscribe if you'd stay up till 3:00 a.m., too."`
   * `"So subscribe if you'd be Eli's ears, too."`

## 2. Exact Hook & Beat Skeleton (Both References Use This)

1. **Hook (`0:00–0:05`):** `"This Minecraft player [weird/extreme stat or habit]."`
2. **Wrong Label (`0:05–0:08`):** `"So the whole server called him [mean label] / thought she was [wrong thing]."`
3. **Open Loop Promise (`0:08–0:12`):** `"But when they found out why, [wholesome whole-server payoff tease]."`
4. **Evidence (`0:12–0:26`):** `"Because [plain explanation of what happened in-game]. So everyone [mocked / put up a sign / built a scoreboard]."`
5. **Human Truth (`0:26–0:40`):** `"But [Name] wasn't [label]. [Short plain IRL truth]. [2–3 simple sentences showing daily life]."`
6. **Cruel Turning Point (`0:40–0:55`):** `"But one day/night, a player [cruel action]. So [Name] logged off and didn't come back."` (+ 1 heartbreaking chat message).
7. **Owner Steps In (`0:55–1:03`):** `"So the owner messaged [Name], and [Name] told him everything. And the owner knew exactly what to do."`
8. **Server Fix & Payoff (`1:03–1:24`):** `[2-sentence Minecraft mechanic or rally] -> [Name logs back in] -> [visible reaction + chat line + counter/chest flip].`
9. **Story CTA (`1:24–1:28`):** `"So subscribe if you'd [tie directly to the story's gesture], too."`

### Reference A: *The Player Who Only Logged On At 3 AM* (87.8s · 256 words)
> This Minecraft player only logged on at 3:00 a.m. and every morning something had changed. So, everyone thought she was a griefer. But when they found out why, the whole server stayed up until 3:00 a.m. Because every morning there was a new book in a chest outside a little kid's house, and it was never signed. So, everyone thought someone was breaking into Leo's base at night. But she wasn't a griefer. She was Leo's mom. She worked night shifts at a hospital. So, she left before Leo got home and came back after he was asleep. Her only free minutes were her break at 3:00 a.m. So, every night she logged on from her phone and wrote Leo a book. And every morning he read it before school. But one night, a player hid by the house, saw a stranger at the chest, and threw the book in lava. "Griefer caught," he typed. So, she logged off and didn't come back. And the next morning, Leo found an empty chest and typed, "Did my mom forget me?" The whole chat went quiet. So, the owner messaged Leo, and Leo told him everything, and the owner knew exactly what to do. So, the next night at 3:00 a.m., the whole server was awake with torches all the way to Leo's house. And when his mom logged on, the chest was full of books, one from every player. And next to it, for the very first time, stood Leo. He had set an alarm. And that night, she wasn't the only one awake at 3:00 a.m. So, subscribe if you'd stay up till 3:00 a.m., too.

### Reference B: *The Player Who Never Heard A Creeper* (87.9s · 253 words)
> This Minecraft player got blown up by creepers 47 times in one week. So the whole server called him the worst player alive. But when they found out why, they rebuilt his house so it could light up. Because a creeper makes one tiny sound before it explodes, a hiss. And when you hear it, you run. But Eli never ran. So the server put a scoreboard at spawn counting every time he blew up, and everyone laughed. But Eli wasn't bad at the game. He was born deaf. He had never heard a creeper in his life. And on the server, nobody could tell. In real life, he was always the kid who missed the joke. Here, he was just a player. But one day, a player led a creeper right to Eli's house just to see if he'd notice. And he didn't. And the house he'd spent a month building was gone. So he logged off and didn't come back. So the owner messaged him, and Eli told him everything. And the owner knew exactly what to do because one block in Minecraft can hear for you, the sculk sensor. When something moves near it, it sends a redstone signal. So every player dug deep underground for sculk sensors. They rebuilt Eli's house and hid them all around it, wired to lamps on every wall. And when Eli logged on, a zombie stepped out of the dark and every lamp lit up. And Eli typed, "I can hear it." And the scoreboard at spawn went back to zero. So subscribe if you'd be Eli's ears, too.
