# Dolphin short — picture and sound board

**Development only, pending script approval.** [Exact narration](narration.md) · [Concept and hook alternatives](../../outlines/ideas-2026-11-18.md)

**Estimated:** 73.9 seconds at 30 fps, 295 spoken words and 36 cue-led shots; target delivery 1080 × 1920. The numbers use `scripts/narration.py` at 240 WPM in a temporary estimate folder. Voice measurements replace these when recorded. Captions are the spoken text only, displayed one uppercase pixel word at a time. The cue in each row points to the exact narration and caption words.

**Cast and set:** the teller (a fisher’s kid), Dad, the dolphin, and two distant search boats. Use the existing avatar, fish, planks, rope/props where available. The film needs a distinct coastal dock, a single lightable half-submerged wreck with an upper cabin, a striped breakaway oar, chest and hatch, two boats and a dolphin rig. The opening and closing use the same dock; the harbor doesn’t have to be rebuilt twice.

**Mood:** clear post-storm daylight with wet wood; green-blue water that stays bright around the hands and chest; a warm stripe of direct light at the open hatch and dock. Use the owner’s In This Town track, under the one voice; water, wood and two fish takes carry the scene turns. The oar’s white crooked stripe, the first fish and the unused chest are the three callbacks to protect through cuts.

## Picture checks for production

- At frame 15, the fish, the reaching kid and the dolphin are distinct when muted. The title is spoken, never an opening card.
- By eight seconds, the empty berth and broken oar establish Dad’s absence. The stripe and Dad’s hand hit before the explanation, by about 0:35.
- The chest is below the water; Dad is in an above-water cabin. Show that connection before the hatch work so the rescue doesn’t look like a new location.
- The blocked hatch, the wedge, the cleared beam and the hand reaching the boat must show contact. No cut that substitutes a reaction for the action.
- The final two fish echo the single fish of the first shot. Finish that gag before the brief subscribe line.
