# What not to do: Monster Egg, version 1

`contact-sheet-every-second.jpg` is the first attempt at this topic: a 20-second film made by an earlier agent under
the base 20-second rules. The owner rejected it: the narration was weak, nothing moved, it opened on a dull shot
outside the school, and then it rushed the ending. The source is on branch `arena/665767a1-kmanincraftghhj22`.

Measured against the treehouse reference:

| | Treehouse (the model) | Monster Egg v1 |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 166.5 s | 20 s |
| Cuts | 86 | **0**. One camera drifts through the whole film |
| Words | 718, in full sentences | 41, in fragments ("My friend's dragon. My sister's phoenix. My worm.") |
| First image | The teller's face, then the whole town | A grey egg on a road, the hero's back to us |
| Lens | Medium shots, subject fills the frame | Wide angle, up to 88°: people are small, sky and road fill the frame |
| World before problem | 34 s of how the town lives | None |
| The ending | Planted at 1:04, paid at 2:36 | Rank 1 → 100 → 10,000 → ∞ in about six seconds |
| Captions | Every word | None |

The lesson is not "make it longer". It is that this kind of story is *explained*: the viewer has to know how the
town works before they can care that one kid's egg is wrong, and has to be asked a question before the answer can
land. Twenty seconds cannot hold that, and one unbroken camera cannot show a new thing for every noun.

The film that replaced it is kept beside this folder with its source and its mp4:
[`../monster-egg/`](../monster-egg/README.md). 93 shots, 746 words, 2:57, and the owner approved it. Read the two
side by side.
