# How we make a short like Monster Village part one

**The evidence.** "In This Town, I Live in a Monster Village" (part one) reached **81% stayed to watch**, the highest
any of our films has had, and it is the film YouTube kept pushing. Part four was **rejected**, and the owner stopped
watching at **1:05**. Both films are the same length, the same narrator, the same speed, the same pack. The
difference is in this file.

This is the operating manual for the **In This Town** topic. `SCRIPT.md` is how the reference channel writes;
`OWNER.md` is the owner's taste; this is what the numbers say about the one film that held an audience. Where they
disagree, follow the one that got 81%.

---

## 1. What was measured

| | Part 1 (81%) | Part 3 ("love it") | Part 4 (rejected) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 2:55 | 2:36 | 2:51 |
| Shots | 74 | 66 | 68 |
| Words / wpm | 713 · 245 | 659 · 254 | 697 · 244 |
| Median shot | 2.37 s | 2.33 s | 2.33 s |
| Lines that open a question | **6** | 6 | **0** |
| Lines where somebody speaks | **10 (14%)** | 13 (20%) | 6 (9%) |
| First 20 s | 5 jokes, 1 question | a sword, a helmet, an arm **that moves** | a guard counting sheep |

Length, speed, cut rate and word count are **not** the difference. Part 4 matches part 1 on every one of them and
still lost the viewer. The difference is what each line *does*.

---

## 2. The thing part 1 does that the others do not

**Every scene is an argument with the viewer, not a report.**

A report says what happened. An argument hands you something you cannot make fit, and lets you carry it.

Part 1 says, in the first 50 seconds: *my mum is a zombie, my dad is a skeleton, a creeper wants hugs, the bus is a
slime, the sun burns monsters but not me, a zombie sat with me in the sun and did not burn, he gave me bread, no
zombie eats bread, he sneezed, monsters do not sneeze, he was like me and I never asked why.*

That is nine oddities and one unanswered question. The viewer cannot stop at 40 seconds, because they are holding
four things that do not add up yet.

Part 4 says: *it was night, half the castle was asleep, one guard woke up, he counted sheep, he went outside, a bush
moved.* That is a sequence. Nothing is owed to the viewer. Everything so far has already been paid.

**The test, for any line:** does this line make the viewer *want* something? If the answer is "no, it tells them
something," the line is scaffolding, and scaffolding is what they leave on.

## 3. The spine

Part 1's real timings, as a percentage of the film. Build to this shape, not to a page count.

| % of film | Time in part 1 | Beat | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 0:00 | **The strange thing, said flat** | "I was the only one who was not a monster." One picture: the odd person among the normal ones |
| 1.5–9% | 0:02.7–0:16 | **Five jokes, 1–3 s each** | Mum/dad/hugs/bus/sun. Each is one small impossible fact, played as ordinary |
| 10% | 0:18 | **The other one** | A character arrives who is *also* odd, in the same way |
| 14% | 0:24 | **First question out loud** | "He just looked at me. A bit too long." The viewer is now working |
| 19–27% | 0:33–0:47 | **Clues, each one funny** | Bread · lantern · sneeze. Every clue is a small joke, so the mystery never feels like homework |
| 27% | 0:47 | **The question named** | "He was like me. I never asked myself why." Naming it doubles the cost of leaving |
| 31% | 0:53 | **Misdirection** | "They locked my door. From the outside." Reads as cruelty for 10 seconds |
| 36% | 1:04 | **Reversal** | "They were standing in a ring… facing out." The cruelty was protection. **This is the retention engine** |
| 40% | 1:10 | **Threat** | Players. The film changes gear and cannot be paused |
| 57% | 1:40 | **The promise paid** | Red blood. The title's question is answered with a picture, not a sentence |
| 65% | 1:53 | **The line the thumbnail sold** | "I am human too" |
| 69% | 2:01 | **The opening line re-read** | "Who do you think carried the basket here?" The first sentence now means something else |
| 74% | 2:09 | **Reversal two** | "They had not come for the village. They had come for him." |
| 77% | 2:15 | **Comic payoff** | Hiss only has to say "Hug" |
| 86–88% | 2:27–2:35 | **Acceptance, then a new stair** | The village takes him back → an army appears |
| 99% | 2:53 | **Cliffhanger, then the ask** | Armour, army, "subscribe for part two" |

**Three things to notice about the shape.**

1. **The first payoff is at 36%, not at 90%.** A viewer gets an answer a third of the way in, which tells them this
   film intends to answer things. After that they trust it with the rest.
2. **Every clue is a joke.** Bread, lantern, sneeze, sun. If a clue is not funny, it is not a clue, it is an
   explanation, and explanations are where people leave.
3. **The last quarter escalates twice.** Acceptance at 86%, then an army at 88%. The film refuses to land softly.

## 4. The rules

### R1. The teller is the subject, always

Part 1's teller is *doing* something in almost every line: hiding stew, running under a sword, standing in front of
his mum. Part 4 spends 36 seconds (0:56–1:32) with the teller in a cage watching a golem fight a ravager, and says
out loud, twice, **"I did not see that either."**

That second sentence is the failure, in the film's own words. A first-person short cannot narrate footage the
narrator was not in. If the teller cannot see it, the film cannot show it — or he has to be *in* it.

- A fight is the teller in the fight, not the teller watching a fight.
- If a beat has to happen elsewhere, cut to the teller reacting to it, and keep him in frame.
- Never write "I did not see this." It tells the viewer the story is not about the person telling it.

### R2. Open a question in the first 20 seconds, and name it by 30%

Part 1 has one open by 0:24 and **names** it at 0:47. Part 4 has none in the whole film. That is the gap between
81% and a stop at 1:05.

A question is: something the viewer notices and cannot close themselves. "He sneezed" is not a question. "He
sneezed, and monsters do not sneeze" is.

### R3. Promise in the first 3 seconds, pay at 60%

The title and the first line are a promise. Part 1's first line promises "I was the only one who was not a
monster." The film pays it at 1:53 with "I am human too," and collects at 2:01 with the basket. Every payoff is a
line the viewer could have predicted but did not.

### R4. Jokes carry the load

Part 1's jokes are not decoration, they are the **pace**. Hiss's hugs, dad's arm, the wet bus: each buys 2–3
seconds of goodwill that the mystery spends later. A film with a mystery and no jokes feels like a test.

### R5. Somebody has to speak

Part 1: 10 lines of dialogue in 74 shots. Part 3: 13. Part 4: 6, and four of them are the teller talking to
himself. Direct speech is what makes the viewer feel people instead of narration. One line of somebody else's voice
every ~15 seconds.

### R6. Do not hand over the footage

No recaps. No "four minutes earlier" **after** the payoff it explains. Part 4 puts its flashback at 1:32, after the
golem fight has already resolved, and the owner stopped at 1:05 — the film spends its middle re-explaining itself.

If a reveal needs a flashback, the flashback goes **before** the thing it explains, and the teller is in it.

### R7. Cut on the word, and change the shot kind every time

`OWNER.md` Rule B: never the same framing twice in a row; every scene has an environment shot, a travelling shot
and an odd angle; the cut lands on the word, not after it. Part 4's rework fixed exactly this and it was still
rejected on story, which tells you the picture rules are necessary but not sufficient.

### R8. End on an open mouth

Every part ends mid-action with a cliffhanger and "subscribe for part N". Part 1 ends: one man in iron, a village
behind him, an army in front. That is a reason to subscribe, not a request.

## 5. What part 4 did, so we never do it again

| Line | The problem |
|---|---|
| "Half of the castle was asleep. The other half was on the night shift." | Two lines of set-dressing before anything is owed |
| "One guard woke up. He could not sleep. He counted sheep." | Three lines about a stranger the viewer has no reason to care about |
| "I was asleep too. So I did not see any of this. They told me later." | The film disowns its own footage |
| "Behind me, somebody fell off a tower. I did not see that either." | Says it again, 30 seconds later |
| 0:56–1:32 | 36 s in which the teller only watches |
| 1:32 "Four minutes earlier" | A flashback placed after the payoff |

**The pattern:** part 4 is written as a *camera* in a village. Part 1 is written as a *boy* in a village. The camera
has nothing at stake.

## 6. Before you write

- [ ] One sentence: what does the viewer **want to know** by the end of the first 15 seconds?
- [ ] What are the **five jokes** in the first 25 seconds? (Part 1 has five. If you have two, you have half a film.)
- [ ] What is the **misdirection** — the thing that reads wrong, then reads right?
- [ ] What is the **first payoff**, and is it before 40%?
- [ ] What does the **first line** mean the second time the viewer hears it?
- [ ] Which line is the **cliffhanger**?

## 7. Before you build

- [ ] The teller is the subject of most lines. Count them. If he is not, rewrite.
- [ ] No line of the form "I did not see this."
- [ ] Every scene has an environment shot, a travelling shot, an odd angle, and a close-up.
- [ ] No two neighbouring shots have the same framing *and* the same camera move.
- [ ] Someone speaks at least every 15 seconds.
- [ ] The first two seconds are the best two seconds of the film (`OWNER.md`).

## 8. After you build: measure the two numbers that matter

1. **Stayed to watch** — if it is under 70%, find the timestamp where it fell below the line and compare that
   stretch against §3. Something in that stretch either reported instead of asked, or left the teller.
2. **The 1:05 test** — the owner stopped part 4 at 1:05. Watch your film from 1:00 to 1:20. If the teller is
   watching somebody else, it will fail there too.

A film that follows this file is not guaranteed 81%. It is guaranteed not to fail the way part 4 failed.
