## In This Town, I Paid a Dolphin to Find My Dad

**Premise:** After a storm, a fisher’s kid finds half of Dad’s striped oar and pays a dolphin with a cod. It leads him to a shipwreck. He thinks it got the job wrong until he sees the other half of the oar coming out of the cabin.

### Hook options for this one story

| Opening | First image and line | Question it creates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fish as payment — selected** | At the dock in clear daylight, the dolphin is already breaching for the cod in the kid’s hand. It takes the fish and dives; the kid grabs the broken red-and-white oar and follows. “In this town, I paid a dolphin to find my dad.” | How does a dolphin find a missing person? The odd action reads before the line ends. |
| The empty dock | The broken oar clacks against Dad’s empty berth. “In this town, half of my dad’s oar came home. He didn’t.” | What happened to Dad? Fast stakes, but the distinctive Minecraft action is delayed. |
| The wrong treasure | Just below the surface, the dolphin stops over a chest, and the kid swims straight past it. “In this town, I left a chest full of treasure at the bottom of the sea.” | Why pass up a chest? A good middle reveal, weaker context at frame one. |

### Rough cut — about 75 seconds

| Time | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:08 | Cod, splash, broken oar. Dad’s berth is empty. One brief flashback shows the kid painting that crooked white stripe on the red oar with him. |
| 0:08–0:25 | The kid follows the dolphin out through the bright harbor. It dives to a wreck on the reef and stops by the chest. The kid thinks that’s as far as it can take him. |
| 0:25–0:39 | He turns to leave. At the waterline the **other half of the striped oar** pokes out from inside the wreck. Let the audience match the stripe before the boy says anything. Dad is alive in the upper cabin. |
| 0:39–1:04 | The cabin hatch is jammed. The kid can’t lift it by hand; he brings the broken oar he carried from the dock back into the action to pry it up. Dad gets out to the boat. The chest stays behind. |
| 1:04–1:15 | Back at the dock, Dad offers the dolphin **two** cod. Repeat the opening composition with the two of them in it. The search ends here, with a small joke instead of a new danger. |

**Game fact / story invention:** Raw cod or salmon can get a dolphin to lead you toward a shipwreck, ocean ruin, or buried treasure. [1](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/heart-sea) In the story Dad reached the wreck during the storm; the dolphin doesn’t have a person-finding power. One pale plank and one red-and-white stripe are enough to attract the eye to the waterline. The upper cabin retains air above the waterline; the story does not rely on an edition-specific underwater door trick.

**Difference check:** who tells it / where / problem / player’s role / end / feeling = **fisher’s kid / reef / missing dad / no outsider / reunion / suspense-to-warmth**. On this branch, the two preceding finished shorts are **Finn / hillside track / runaway delivery / no outsider / tip / deadpan** (Cake) and **Finn / kitchen and restaurant / chores and a job test / no outsider / hired / escalating comedy** (Dishwasher). The new story also avoids the odd-mob-child/players-at-night template the owner flagged in the topic films.

Check that the stripe and oar halves match on a phone screen and that the wreck and oar pay off before 0:39.
