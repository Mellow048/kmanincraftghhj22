In this town, the new kid never took off his helmet.
Not in class. Not at lunch. Not even in the pool.
The teacher asked him once. He just shook his head.
Nobody knew his face. Nobody knew where he lived.
He sat at the back, next to me. He never said a word.
I thought he was just shy.
Then the first kid went missing.
His desk was empty on Monday. His bag was still under it.
The adults said he ran away.
On Wednesday, another desk was empty. On Friday, two more.
Posters went up on every wall. Nobody played outside anymore.
And I noticed something.
Every kid who went missing had been mean to him.
One threw his lunch. One knocked on his helmet. One tried to pull it off.
I told my mom. She said, "Do not blame the new kid."
I told the teacher. He said, "Sit down."
So I watched him myself.
Every day after school, he walked into the woods. Alone.
Every morning, he came back. And his helmet was a little wet.
One day he turned and looked right at me.
I could not see his eyes. But I knew he saw me.
He wrote something on a paper. He slid it onto my desk.
It said, "Do not follow me."
I followed him.
That night, I hid behind a tree at the edge of the woods.
He was standing under the old lamp. He was not alone.
Three big kids had him in a circle. The last three bullies in town.
They pushed him. He did not push back.
"Take it off," one said. "Show us your face."
He held his helmet with both hands. He was shaking all over.
He was not angry. He was scared. But not of them.
Two of them held his arms. The third one pulled.
The helmet came off.
There was no face.
There was a hole. And something inside it moved.
One tentacle came out. Then five. Then too many to count.
They were as thick as trees. They grabbed all three kids.
Nobody screamed. There was no time.
It pulled them in. All three. And it swallowed.
Then it was quiet.
The kid picked up his helmet. His hands were still shaking.
I took one step back.
A stick snapped under my foot.
He stopped.
One tentacle turned around. Slowly. It pointed at my tree.
I held my breath. I did not move.
It came closer. It touched the bark, right next to my face.
