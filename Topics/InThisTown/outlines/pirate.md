## What was added (cut any of these and the story is still his)

1. **The reason for the rule**, in full sentences, in the first three lines. Boats that pass the last buoy do not come back, so the boats stay chained until eighteen. He has called a rule with no reason a plot hole. The kraken is what that sentence was talking about. It is not named until he sees it.
2. **A door he calls a boat, which sinks in the pond.** One joke, so the years of waiting are a picture and not a caption that says "years later". Cut the two lines and the key still lands.
3. **The ball is red, and it is packed in the chest** he later opens. He said a ball was fine. The chest is the same chest, so the ball does not appear from nowhere.
4. **No names.** The two villagers are told apart by a straw hat and a bow. The straw hat is the one who goes under the floor and jumps. The player brings a rope. He is a friend. He is not a traitor, and he does not know anything the others do not.
5. **The key.** On the morning he turns eighteen, his dad puts the key in his hand. That is the join between "no boat until 18" and "they set sail".
6. **The one in the water sees the shadow first**, because he is facing the boat and the other three have their backs to the sea. Then they turn, slowly, as asked. He is still in the water when the kraken comes up, so part 2 has someone to get back. If the climb-back should happen in part 1, say so and the two lines come out.
7. **A wait with no words on the turn.** The helmet unmasking felt empty when the picture did not move. The turn is its own clips so the pause can sit between them. No new sentence.
8. **Picture only, easy to delete: two seconds of the night shadow before the first word**, then the dock on "In this town". This is the hook fix used after he stopped Monster Village part 4 at 1:05. The face of the kraken is not in it. The ending is still the first time we see what the shadow is. Say "dock first" and it goes.

Not added: a secret about the player, a death, a second monster, a lesson, a recap.

## Length

Short cut, on purpose. 437 spoken words, 41 shots. At the pace he approved (about 235 words a minute, with a quarter to three quarters of a second between steps) the voice is about 1:52, and the pauses bring the film to about 1:56. Under the two-minute wall, with a few seconds to spare if a line runs long. Not the 2:55 of Monster Village part 1. That film's long sit is what a stay-graph dies in. This one has no sit. Day and night are shots, not a montage.

The slow turn is not bought by speeding the voice, and it is not a silent minute. "Slowly" is its own shot, with a pause on either side, and then the arms come up one at a time. If he wants that turn held longer than the words, say so. We add the hold only up to two minutes, or we move the film up a cut. We do not speed it.

## The pictures that have to be true before a yes

- **Frame one of the spoken film** is the map, big, in his hands, and past it the chained boat he is already reaching for. A hand stops him. Not a wide shot of a town. Not the sea, empty.
- **The two seconds before that**, if kept, are night, the boat small, a shadow already in the water behind it, moving. No face. No words.
- **The turn is slow, and it is night**, as asked. The lantern is the only light. The kraken is not a speck on the horizon and not a still drawing: one arm comes up, then the head, and the eye is level with the lantern, so a phone can read it with no sound.
- **The subscribe line sits on the kraken**, not on a black card. The monster is still the picture.
- Captions sit at the chest, not in the bottom band a Shorts frame covers.

## What to copy from Monster Village part 1, and what not to

Copy the machine, not the plot. A rule for the whole town. One kid it fails to stop. A thing you can see early (the ball in the chest, the chain, the buoy) that comes back. A moment where everybody is looking the wrong way. Then the picture, bigger than the line.

Do not copy the five-person introduction, the long sit on the wall, or a last shot that is only a sign. Those are the places a 2:55 film loses the people who already stayed.
