In this town, the new kid never took off his helmet. And now I knew why.
I was behind a tree. I was not breathing.
Something cold touched my face. It was wet.
It felt my cheek. My nose. My eyes. Like somebody reading in the dark.
I waited for it to pull me in.
It did not. It stopped. And it went back.
I heard a click. The helmet was on again.
Then I heard something worse. He was crying.
I looked round the tree. He was sitting under the lamp, with his arms round his knees.
I should have run. I still do not know why I did not.
"Are you going to take me too?" I said.
He shook his head. He picked up a stick. And he wrote in the dirt.
I DO NOT WANT TO.
Then he wrote some more. IT IS NOT ME. IT LIVES IN HERE.
The helmet was not there to hide his face. It was a lid.
"Why not me?" I said.
He wrote, YOU WERE NEVER MEAN TO ME.
It only woke up when somebody hurt him.
He had lived in four towns. It had happened in four towns.
"Where are they?" I said. "The kids?"
He did not write anything. He put his hand on the helmet.
So I put my ear on it.
Far away, inside, somebody was knocking. Tok. Tok. Tok.
They were still in there.
The next morning, he sat at the back, next to me. Same as always.
I told nobody. Who would I tell? I already knew what they would say. Sit down.
At lunch, I gave him half of my bread. He could not eat it. He held it anyway.
It was the first time I saw him nod.
Then the bell rang. And we walked out to the gate.
Six big kids were waiting there. The brothers of the missing ones.
Somebody had seen three bullies follow him into the woods.
They had a rope. They were not looking at him. They were looking at his helmet.
I looked at him.
His helmet was already wet.
