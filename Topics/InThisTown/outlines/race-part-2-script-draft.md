# The Fastest Kid Wins, part 2: script draft 1 (NOT approved, no voice recorded)

whole story below is the agent's.** What is his, from his notes on part 1: there are at least three parts; at every

About 400 words, about 1:40.

In this town, the fastest kid wins a trophy. And I had just come second.
I was happy about it. For about one minute.
Then I saw the winner's shoes. Red. With a green light. The same as mine.
He saw mine too. He stopped smiling.
"Where did you get those?" he said.
"A witch," I said. "In the forest. At night."
"Purple hat? Very glad to get rid of them?"
"Yes."
"Same witch," he said.
He had won this race last year. And the year before.
Nobody had ever asked how.
"How many buttons did you find?" he said.
"Three," I said.
He laughed. "There are four."
He showed me. It was under the heel. Small, and black.
"What does it do?" I said.
"I do not know. I have never pressed it. And you should not."
Then the others came in. The baby was fourth. It looked very angry.
We all rested by the river. We drank water. Nobody talked to the baby.
Then the horn went. Stage two. The desert.
Three. Two. One. Go!
The sand was hot, and deep, and slow. Everybody was slow.
Except him. He bent down. He pressed the second button.
And he was gone. All I saw was a line of dust.
I was not going to lose to my own shoes.
So I pressed the second button too.
I went through a cactus. Then another cactus. I was fine. The cactus was not.
And I caught him. We ran side by side, across the whole desert.
Then I smelled something. Smoke.
It was his shoes. The green light had turned red.
"I cannot stop!" he shouted. "They will not turn off!"
He went past the rest camp. He went past the flag. He kept going, over the dunes.
I stopped at the flag. I was first.
I had never been first at anything. And nobody was there to see it.
I sat down in the shade, and I waited.
Then somebody sat down next to me.
A purple hat.
"Hello again," said the witch. "Has he pressed the fourth one yet?"
I looked at the dunes. Far away, there was a very big flash.
"I think he just did," I said.
