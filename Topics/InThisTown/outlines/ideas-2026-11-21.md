## The one pitch

**`In This Town, Nobody Made a Sound`** — a town that does not speak, and a kid who cannot stop hiccups.

**Frame one:** mid-morning in an ordinary overworld town. Buildings, roofs, and doors in warm daylight. The camera
pushes in slowly — through the street — until it rests on a kid crouched at the edge of the road, looking at a
large, round field in front of him. First sound in the whole film: **a hiccup**. The town goes polite, quiet —
people turn their heads — and the frame stays put.

**Line 1:** "In this town, nobody made a sound."

**The setup (0:04–0:16):** the town is built as if it belongs to a ghost. Wool spread on every floor; the bell is
wrapped in a blanket; the school uses sign language; no windows are open; nobody walks; nobody laughs; even the
crowds of the school feel like towns under weight. Captions are the only words.

**The teller (0:16–0:30):** the kid. Details: a villager-kid at noon, crouched down beside a bell-tower; let him
look at the truth — **he is the only one awake in the whole town.** The teller's first small joke: none of the
townspeople say hello. They don't make a sound, and they don't look. The kid doesn't know why he still hiccups.
He has become used to it.

**The viewer-ahead rule (0:30–0:46):** the town keeps itself quiet because **something below the town hears**.
The viewer has never made a sound since frame one; the town has not made a sound in years; the kid is not quiet;
he is the only person in the town who makes a sound, and the only one who wants somebody to hear him.

**The plants and pay-offs (carried whole):**
- The wool-floor, the bell in blanket, the muted sign-language school — looked at by the teller at the beginning,
  then **asked back to himself** at the reveal when the whole thing rearranges around the truth.
- The hiccups — **the only sound the whole story needs** — they are the tell. Every hiccup is the viewer's
  cue that something inside the kid matters.
- The kid's grandmother — last rung of the town's memory — she hears their bell every night when nobody else
  is in town. She is not a mother; she is **the quiet one** who rings a bell that is wrapped in a blanket —
  she rings it anyway.
- The kid's name — **he is the only one who knows why nobody talks** — and the one thing he tries to fix at the end.

**The reveal (1:01–1:20):** the teller realizes the whole hush is not about him. The town is quiet **because of
the thing below** — a thing blind and hunts by sound. The kid's hiccup is not the thing the town is afraid of.
**The town's silence is a keeping of its ears** — not to hush the kid, but because the warden's silence is loud
when it's inside the town — and the town has to be loud in secret every night, which is exactly what his hiccups
do.

**The rule that looks unkind and is love:** the whole town fears the one kid who cannot shut up. He thinks that's
the reason everyone is so quiet — he thinks the town is keeping him in silence. The reveal is that they are
keeping **the warden** in silence. The dead love the hidden sound. **The kid's hiccups are nobody's fear — they
are the one voice the warden can hear from below,** which means the noise is not the bad thing it looks like.

**The button (2:20):** "Like and subscribe. In this town, nobody made a sound. But **I am not allowed to be quiet**
— I was not built to be quiet. The warden hears me every night."

**One sentence:** A hushed village of quiet people who cannot stop making a sound, with a kid who barely speaks;
silence is the thing that starts, the thing that's kept, and the whole story is about the fact that being heard
can be the same as being kept safe.

## Natural script (2026-11-21, re-edited for flow)

Frame one: mid-day, ordinary overworld path. A kid crouches in the grass. First sound in the whole film: his hiccup.
One deliberate pause only — right before the bell. Everyone else speaks at full speed.

```narration
In this town, nobody made a sound. And I was the kid who could not help it.

The floors were covered in wool. The school was in signs — nobody talked, nobody hummed, and the bell was wrapped up in a black blanket.

I was the only one awake. Everybody went to bed at noon — and I hiccupped. Every day.

So I tried to stop. I could not. I thought they were quiet because of me.

But one night, the bell tower door opened. It was the grandmother. She was ringing the bell — not for anybody. Just for the thing below.

So I asked her. Why is this town so quiet? She said something below the town hears everything. And if we make too much noise... it wakes up.

I held my breath. The whole town did too.

Then I lifted the bell — and rung it loud. And they all listened.

Like and subscribe. I still hiccup. And the town is still quiet.
```

**Voice:** voice-02 (male, conversational, energetic). **Status:** draft for approval — not built. Preview: `.scratch/narration-preview.mp3`.

**Why this shape works:**
- One frame-one sound — the hiccup — not a speech; pure emotion at 0:00.
- Every plant pays off in the payoff: wool → hushed listening, bell-in-blanket → the bell ringing, "quiet because of me" → the viewer knows it's because of the thing below.
- One deliberate pause only — the "..." in "if we make too much noise... it wakes up." That's the one moment the viewer is ahead of the kid.
- Whole-film emotion is silence + sound, not love and fight. A new shape, not a mob-family hug.
