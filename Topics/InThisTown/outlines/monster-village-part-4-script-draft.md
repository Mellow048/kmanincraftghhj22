# I Live in a Monster Village, part 4: script draft 1 (NOT approved, no voice recorded)

Asked for by the owner 2026-11-14 ("then give me a script for the 2 shorts"). **He gave no outline for this part, so the
whole story below is the agent's.** It pays off what part 3 planted: the cages, Mo not moving, the guard who laughed,
mum's plan, the creeper that nearly went off, dad's arm, the iron golem everybody is afraid of, and "nobody hates
players more than a pillager".

About 430 words, about 1:45.

In this town, I was the only one who was not a monster. And I was in a cage.
My friend Mo was in the next one. He still was not moving.
The guard who laughed at me was asleep on a chair. His keys were on his belt.
I could not reach them. I had stopped trying to reach things.
Then the sun went down. And I heard something outside the wall.
It was forty monsters, trying to walk quietly. They were not good at it.
My mum's plan had three parts.
Part one. The pillagers knocked on the front gate. With an axe.
Every guard in the castle ran to the front.
Part two. The monsters went round to the back.
There was no door at the back. But there was a creeper. The one who nearly went off.
"Now?" he said.
"Now," said my mum.
He had never been so happy.
Then there was a door.
Part three was my dad's arm.
It came in through the hole, on its fingers. It went up the chair. It took the keys.
The guard woke up. He saw an arm holding his keys. He did not laugh this time.
It opened my cage. Click. It opened Mo's cage. Click.
"Mo!" I said. He opened one eye.
"Is that an arm?" he said.
"It is my dad's," I said.
"Okay," said Mo.
We ran for the hole. My mum was there. My dad was there. I had never been so happy to see a skeleton.
Then the ground shook.
Everybody stopped. The monsters. The pillagers. Even the players.
It came out of the tower gate. The iron golem.
It was as tall as a house. It looked at forty monsters.
Forty monsters looked at the floor.
Then it looked at me. It walked right up to me. I shut my eyes.
Nothing happened. I opened them.
It was holding out a flower.
"It thinks you are a village kid," whispered Mo. "Take it. Take it!"
I took the flower. The golem sat down. And the fight was over.
We had won! The monsters cheered. I cheered.
Then the chief of the pillagers walked over. He was not cheering.
"The castle is ours," he said. "That was the deal."
"Yes," said my mum.
He pointed at me. Then at Mo.
"And those two are players. Nobody said anything about players."
Twenty crossbows went up. Click. Click. Click.
