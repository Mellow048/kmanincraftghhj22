# In This Town, I Keep My Mum in the Light

> **In this town, I keep my mum in the light. She is a spider.**

**One sentence:** A boy whose mum is a spider can only have her while the lamps are on — so he stops sleeping to keep
them lit, and the thing that comes for him for not sleeping is the thing only the dark version of her can kill.

**Topic:** `in-this-town`, `cut: "short"`. Six beats. 108 s, 52 shots, 432 words.

## The six words (his test, `OWNER.md` §5)

| | His last two films (creeper / enderman) | This |
|---|---|---|
| Who tells it | a kid | **a kid** ← same |
| Where | a town of one famous mob | a village of villagers who are afraid of her |
| What goes wrong | nobody can touch him / he cannot look at her | she stops being his mum when the light goes out |
| What the player is | six raiders, at night, for a mob's drop | three phantoms, at night, that he made himself by not sleeping |
| How it ends | mum leans on him / he sees her face | she saves him and does not know she did, and he says nothing |
| How it feels | funny, then warm | funny, then quiet |

**One row the same out of six.** The adoption is gone too: he is their son, and nobody in the house has ever
discussed how that works — which is both the joke and the last line of the film.

## Frame one (0.0 s), and the first cut

Bright noon, the middle of a village street. A boy of nine holds a lit lantern up in both hands at chest height,
looking at us, entirely unsurprised. Inside the take: **a black leg** comes round the corner of the house at 0.6 s,
**a second** at 0.9 s, then her body and **eight eyes** at 1.4 s, and at 2.0 s **she takes the lantern out of his
hands with one leg** and holds it over her own head.

That is the whole premise, mute, in two seconds: a boy, a spider, and the boy is carrying the light *for* her.
First cut at **2.1 s**. One take, four arrivals, no fast cuts — exactly the opening shape measured in the study.

## Why this one, on the study's terms

- **It is the engine and not the spine.** A kid, a mum, one famous mob, one physical rule, a vanilla threat, a list,
  an image. What changes is everything the owner has already been shown twice: no adoption, no mob town, no players,
  no hug, no night raid. The town is villagers who are frightened of his mother, so the outsider is the *family*, not
  just the boy — a pressure no film in the catalogue has.
- **The threat is caused by his love.** The hits had players arriving for loot, which is a coincidence of the plot.
  Here: he keeps the light on → he cannot sleep → the phantoms come. Every beat is the previous beat's fault, which is
  what `MASTERPROMPT.md` §3 asks for and what most of the 2k films do not have.
- **The spider is an iconic mob nobody has used as family.** `Spider` is already a pack part (`monsters.tsx`) with
  `scarf`, `hat`, `mood` and `scale`, so the mum and the cave-spider dad are free: dad is the same part at 0.6 scale
  in blue.
- **The last line hands the viewer a rule.** "If your street goes dark, do not run. Stand still." That is the kind of
  line that gets repeated in comments, and it costs nothing to shoot.
- **It is cheap.** One new mob part (the phantom), four props, one street. The study's own finding: the three 20k
  films are the cheapest sets in the catalogue, and this stays that way.

## The risks, honestly

1. **A spider is not obviously cute.** The film lives or dies on her reading as a mum in frame one. *Mitigation: a pink
   apron and a bow (the part already takes `scarf` and `hat`), knitting as her first action, and no fast leg movement
   in daylight — she moves like furniture until the dark.*
2. **The spider's rule is less famous than "creepers run from cats".** *Mitigation: it is stated flat in two lines
   (`2.rule`, `2.dark`) and shown three times before the threat, which is more than the hits spent on any rule.*
3. **The back half is at night.** The study found dark frames are the single biggest view-killer. *Mitigation: one warm
   lamp within a metre of a face in every night shot; the enderman film carries 2.4% dark frames and held 20k; the
   target here is under 2%.*
4. **"A monster parent saves him" could feel like the third version of the same turn.** *Mitigation: they never touch,
   there is no reunion and no hug, and the film's last beat is a rule for the viewer, not a feeling. The `6.ask` joke
   keeps it dry.*
5. **Phantoms are a thin, blue, meme-ish mob.** *Mitigation: they are only ever seen as silhouettes against the moon,
   slowly circling, and only one of them dives. Nobody has to draw a phantom well if it is never lit.*
6. **The one loose fact: in vanilla, spiders do not hunt phantoms.** The film never claims it — she attacks what comes
   at her son, in the dark, on her own ground, which is what a spider in the dark does (`2.dark`). But a viewer who
   knows the game could argue with it. *If he wants it airtight, the threat becomes the village's iron golem instead:
   golems really do attack spiders on sight, the golem is the village's own protection, and the film turns into
   runner-up C with this family. That version is mechanically perfect and it loses the "he made the threat himself"
   cause and effect. His call.*

## Runners-up, three lines each

**The gold helmet.** Dad is a piglin; he is only peaceful while the boy wears gold, and the boy has outgrown the
helmet. *Limitation:* what he wears. *Danger:* his own father, the day it comes off. Costs a whole new world (the
Nether) and a piglin model — the most expensive of the three, and the funniest.

**The golem outside.** A monster family in a village that keeps two iron golems "for safety"; the golems love the
human boy and attack his parents on sight. *Limitation:* distance. *Danger:* the family's own fence. The golem part
already exists (`villagers.tsx`), so this is the cheapest of all — but its turn is a dilemma rather than an image,
which is a harder thing to land in 108 seconds.
