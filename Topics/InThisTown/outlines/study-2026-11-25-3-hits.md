# Study: the three 20k films, and what the other thirty-seven are missing

**Read 2026-11-25.** For the owner's standing goal: *"get out of 2k views jail"*.
Nothing here is a pitch. It is the measurement that any pitch now has to pass.

Read to write it: the three films' **shot logs, scripts and contact sheets** (`20k-hits/01`, `02`, `03`), every
`README.md` in `Topics/InThisTown/reference/`, `Topics/InThisTown/WHY-IT-WORKED.md`,
`reference/monster-village/NEW-SHORT.md`, `reference/never-seen-my-mums-face/README.md`,
`reference/README.md`, `OWNER.md`, `MASTERPROMPT.md`, `docs/WHY-THE-3-HITS-BLEW-UP.md`,
`docs/VIRAL-SHORTS-BLUEPRINT.md`, and the `timing.json` and `scene*.tsx` of **all 41 films in `src/videos/` that have
a clock** (measured fresh today, not quoted).

---

## 1. The uncomfortable result

I measured every film in the repository on the things `scripts/film-metrics.py` and the checkers can see. The three
20k films are **inside the normal range on every single one of them**. Some of them they are on the *slow* side of it.

| | Monster Village 1 | Creeper Parents | Enderman Parents | The other 38 films |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outcome | 18k+ | 20k+ | 20k+ | one ~2k, most 1.4–1.5k |
| Length | 175.0 s | 100.4 s | 105.6 s | 53.7 – 176.7 s |
| Shots | 74 | 50 | 52 | 20 – 99 |
| Median hold | **2.37 s** | **2.03 s** | **1.97 s** | 1.47 – 2.83 s |
| Longest hold | 4.93 s | 4.10 s | 4.07 s | 2.57 – 5.93 s |
| Words a minute | 244 | 240 | 235 | 161 – 262 |
| Words a shot | 9.6 | 8.0 | 7.9 | 4.6 – 10.9 |
| Places (zones) | **2** | **2** | **2** | 1 – 9 |
| Opening luma (archived) | 101.0 | 152.5 | 121.6 | — (not re-measured) |

**The fastest films in the catalogue are not the hits.** `everyone-swims-except-me` is 99 shots at a 1.47 s median.
`i-traded-my-bed-for-a-plate-of-stew` is 69 shots at 1.47 s. `my-brother-got-bitten` is 72 at 1.62. The two 20k
parents films sit at 2.03 s — slower than the typical film in this repository, which is 1.7–1.9 s. Cutting faster is
not the lever, and it has not been for months.

**The hits are also the cheapest films in the catalogue.** All three happen in **one street**: 74 of Monster Village's
74 shots, 49 of the creeper film's 50, 50 of the enderman film's 52. The others are a photograph and a front room.
Meanwhile `the-barber-who-never-went-to-parties` uses 8 places and changes set 18 times, and
`a-stranger-moved-into-my-house` uses 4 and changes set 18 times. Building more sets has been the trend; it is the
opposite of what the winners do.

So: the numbers every review pass has been optimising are **saturated**. A 2k film and a 20k film are
indistinguishable on length, cut rate, words a minute, words a shot and set count. The gap has to be in what is
*inside* the two seconds, and that is the one thing no checker in this repo measures.

## 2. The one thing that separates them: 3 out of 3

Every hit's first two lines and the second nine seconds.

| Film | Line 1 | The family, by | Where he came from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monster Village 1 | "In this town, I was the only one who was not a monster." | 0:02.7 mum, 0:03.8 dad | 0:05.0 "a boy they found in a basket" |
| Creeper Parents | "In this town, I am the only cat." | 0:04.4 mum, 0:04.4 dad | 0:06.5 "They found me in a box, as a kitten." |
| Enderman Parents | "In this town, I have never seen my mum's face." | 0:00.0 mum, 0:07.8 dad | 0:10.4 "Once, a baby. That was me." |

**All three are the same product: a human-ish kid, adopted, whose mum and dad are a famous hostile mob.** Not a town
rule. Not a power. A *family*, named out loud, inside nine seconds, that should not be possible.

Seventeen films, and not one of them about a **mum and dad**. The three films that broke 20k are the three films about
a mum and dad.

This is the finding, and it is worth being blunt about: **the channel's winning product was "my monster mum and dad",
and the catalogue stopped making it on 2026-11-16.** The five auto-mode films were built the next day on the owner's
instruction to be *less* alike; the likeness was removed from the wrong end. What he objected to was that the enderman
film had the creeper film's **spine** — the same six beats, same night raid, same hug. What he never objected to was
the family, and the family is the engine.

A family is what every hit has. The second act is what must change.

## 3. What the three do that no checker can see

Six things, each with the cue it lives at, so a new film can be held against them.

**1. The first shot is one take, 1.6–2.8 s, and things arrive inside it.**
The first cut in the three hits lands at **1.63 s**, **2.43 s** and **2.73 s** — not at 0.4 s. There are no fast cuts
at the start. What there is, is arrival: in `1.town` the green figure appears at 0.40 s, the skeleton at 0.60 s, and
by the word "monster" (2.108 s) the crowd fills the space around the boy's head. `NEW-SHORT.md` counts **eleven
picture changes in two seconds** inside that single shot.
*The rule: the opening is one moving take with at least three things entering it, and the first cut is late.*

**2. One physical limitation of the parent, stated early, used four times, and used last.**
Creepers cannot hug (no arms). Endermen cannot be looked at. Zombies and skeletons burn in the sun.
Each film states it by 0:12, uses it as a joke three times, and then the **last ten seconds are that limitation
being solved** — a lean (`6.arms`), a pumpkin (`6.up`). Nothing else in the film has to be invented.

**3. The teller believes something wrong, and the viewer can see past him.**
Monster Village: "I thought it was because I was the weak one" (0:55) → the ring of villagers is **facing out**
(1:04). Creeper: "They love me very much. From the other side of the room" → his mum is the one who walks up
(`6.step`). Enderman: "I thought he did not like humans" → the stare was recognition.
`WHY-IT-WORKED` names it exactly: *the pleasure is the two beats where you are smarter than the narrator.* Every
film since has a narrator who is right about everything, and so has nothing to discover.

**4. Three to five boring plants, restated in one list, then re-read.**
The list is the cheapest emotional hit in the form: "The bread. The lantern. The sneeze. The sun." then *"Who do you
think carried the basket here?"* Enderman has seven (knees, photo, kitchen, drawing, scream, puddle, pumpkin) and pays
them at `6.knees`, `7.eyes`, `8.photo`, `8.kitchen`. A plant must be *boring* where it goes in — a hug nobody wants, a
loaf of bread, a photo where he looks at his shoes.

**5. The threat is vanilla, armed, and arrives after the family is loved.**
Players with swords and sacks, at night, after a mob's drop — gunpowder, pearls, XP — at **0:42–0:48** in both short
hits. Not a made-up monster, not a metaphor. The viewer already knows what a Steve with a diamond sword means.

**6. The ending is an image, and the ask is a joke.**
"Or I will come and say hello." / "But do not stare. My dad is right behind you." / "Like and subscribe for part
two", over a friend putting on armour. The subscribe line is the last laugh, not a request.

## 4. The misses, in the owner's own words

Every line below is from `OWNER.md` or the reference pages, not from me:

- Monster Village **part 2** opened at 0.0 s inside a dark night pig farm without shaders → "the retention is bad,
  the hook needs work, overall it was downgraded".
- Part 4: he **stopped watching at 1:05**. What he named: the jaw that was not open, a tower with no floors, the
  picture moving *after* the line said it had, a frame of grey smoke blocks — "i have no idea what im looking at
  without context", "the fight was boring … it lacked everything".
- The five auto-mode films, when all 323 shots were re-looked at: four of them fell short in the same place —
  **"the first two seconds must keep changing"**; two opened on one finished picture and held it; one opened on the
  teller *in profile*.
- `same-face`: correctly built, but "every character is a brown Villager skin … which looks plain compared to
  instantly recognizable Creepers, Endermen, Zombies".
- Invented town rules (*"nobody looks at clocks"*, *"everyone whispers on Tuesdays"*) cost the viewer **fifteen
  seconds** of comprehension; Minecraft lore costs one.
- One of the five is **told in the present tense** and parts of two more are, against a benchmark that is all past
  tense — noted, not fixed, because it needs a re-record.

None of those are pacing faults. Every one is a fault in **what the shot is of**.

## 5. What I cannot prove, and will not pretend to

- **There are no per-film view numbers in this repository** except the three hits, the two Superpowers (about 1.3k,
  63–65%), and "one other around 2k and most around 1.4–1.5k" (2026-11-18, film unnamed). So the table in §2 is
  **correlation on a sample of one channel**, and the honest reading is: *the only films we know the outcome of, and
  that had a mum and dad, are the three that broke 20k; the only films we know the outcome of, and that did not, had
  no family.*
- **82% stayed to watch is one data point.** A Short's distribution is also volume, timing and luck; nothing here
  explains the algorithm, and `MASTERPROMPT.md` says not to invent explanations of it.
- **Luma and hook were not re-measured today** (no `ffmpeg` and no `numpy` in this sandbox). The opening-luma numbers
  quoted are the archived ones from `20k-hits/`, and they are consistent with the archive's own measurement notes.
- **Nobody has listened to any mix**, including the three hits, after the fact.

What is *not* in doubt: the three hits' shot logs, scripts, cue times and set counts, because they are the delivered
source and I read all of them, and the other 38 films' shot counts and clocks, because I measured them today.

## 6. The card: twelve checks, before a render

Written so each one can be answered from three stills and `timing.json`, by me or by whoever builds next. No item
depends on taste.

| # | Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The premise, in frame one | One still at 0.0 s: a person **and** a mob **and** something that cannot be true. Sound off, phone size. |
| 2 | Arrivals inside the first take | ≥3 things enter or change in the first shot; the first cut is **1.6–2.8 s** |
| 3 | Line 1 | ≤10 words, starts with "I" or "my", states an impossible fact, and is the title |
| 4 | The family | mum or dad named aloud by **0:09**, and what they are |
| 5 | The limitation | one physical rule of the parent, stated by **0:12**, broken/joked 3 times, **solved in the last 10 s** |
| 6 | The wrong belief | a sentence where the teller is wrong; the picture corrects him **within 25 s**; both cues written down |
| 7 | The list | 3–5 plants, restated inside 8 s near 75% of the runtime, then one line that re-reads them |
| 8 | The threat | vanilla, armed, arrives **after** 0:40, and is not invented |
| 9 | The last picture | an image, not a sentence; the subscribe line is a joke |
| 10 | Length | **100–115 s**. (75% of 1:45 is 1:19 of attention; the same viewer clears 75% at 1:50 — the owner's own plan) |
| 11 | Sets | **1 street + 1 interior**. More than 3 places is a warning, not a virtue |
| 12 | Sound | 100–127 effects a minute; music `none` for the 2–3 s before the reveal; voice about 14 dB over the bed |

And the gate from `OWNER.md` §5 that comes *before* any of it: **six words for the new idea and six for each of the
last two films** — who tells it, where, what goes wrong, what the player is, how it ends, how it feels. More than two
the same as either film, and it is not a new idea.

## 7. Three spines that keep the engine and change everything else

Each is a **family of one famous mob with a human kid**, and each changes the thing the last two films shared: the
limitation, the sense, and who the danger is. None of these is a pitch yet — they are the three shapes that pass §6.

**A. The gold helmet.** Dad is a piglin. He is only peaceful while the kid is wearing gold, so the kid has worn the
same helmet since he was four, and it no longer fits. *Limitation:* what he wears. *Danger:* not players — his own
father, on the day the helmet comes off. *List:* the helmet, the barter, the gold nuggets in his sock, the door he is
not allowed to open. Real mechanics: piglins are neutral to players in gold armour, barter, and turn hostile without
it. **A family whose love is conditional on an item** is a new joke: every hug is a transaction.

**B. The town under the warden.** Everyone in the town is silent, because the warden under it is blind and hunts by
sound. The kid has never heard his mum's voice and she has never heard his. *Limitation:* sound. *Danger:* a dropped
block. *List:* the sand on the floor, the bell nobody rings, his shoes off at the door, the sound he makes once.
Formally new for this channel: the film is nearly silent under the narration, which is the opposite of the 127-effect
sheets. Real mechanic: the warden detects vibration; wool blocks sound.

**C. The golem outside.** A monster family that has bought two iron golems "for safety", like a fence. The golems
attack any monster on sight, and they like the human kid — so the one thing that keeps him safe is the thing that will
kill his parents, and he is the one who has to choose. *Limitation:* proximity. *Danger:* the family's own fence.
Real mechanics: golems attack hostile mobs on sight; they hold poppies.

Each of these is cheap: one street, one family, one prop that becomes the ending.

## 8. What I would do next, in order

1. Pick one of the three spines (or say "none of these" and I will write three to the card instead).
2. I write the **script only** — 100–115 s, six beats, with the card filled in line by line: which cue states the
   limitation, which line is the wrong belief, where the list lands, what the last picture is.
3. He reads it. That is the stop. `AGENTS.md` and his own rule: script approved before a set is built.
4. Then one film, built to the card, measured against §1's table, and the card's numbers put in its `REVIEW.md` —
   so the next film inherits a *test*, not an opinion.

The one thing I would not do is build a sixth film on the 100–145 s, 50–75 shot, one-rule-one-town model. That model
is what most of the last twenty films are, and it is where 1.4k lives.
