## What this batch does differently

The last five were told by a dog, two grown villagers, a trader and a mob nobody chose. None was a kid, and only the
dog had a family. The one he found weakest is the one with the least to feel: a grown villager lists what was done to
him and loses. (That reading is the agent's guess. He did not say what it lacks.)

Monster Village part 1, the film with 82% stayed to watch, is a **kid, a family and a secret**. So this batch goes back
to that, and stays away from the outline he called "kinda similar": the odd kid in a town of one mob, six players at
night, his weakness saves the town, mum.

Kept in all five, from the Monster Village study ([`../reference/monster-village/README.md`](../reference/monster-village/README.md)):
"I" and the past tense from the first word; a thing the teller has wrong and says out loud; things the viewer can work
out before he does; his own try fails; the clues said back as a list; a second, bigger truth; an ending that opens.
Every turn is a true Minecraft fact.

## 1. *In This Town, My Brother Got Bitten*

> In this town, my brother got bitten. Everybody said he was gone. He was in our cellar. And he still knocked twice.

**Frame one:** a villager kid sits on the cellar stairs holding a lantern. Behind iron bars, a green villager in his
brother's clothes holds out a wooden toy sword, handle first.

**The story.** The rule in this town: someone who is bitten is put outside the gate before sunrise. "We do not watch."
His big brother Tom was bitten pulling him in through the door. Dad carried Tom out. That night, two knocks from
under the floor: Tom's knock. Dad had put him in the cellar instead, the one with iron bars and a bed that the kid
always thought was where bad kids went. The neighbours have habits. The priest sells to some families at half price
and will not say why. Old Mrs Hale never goes out without her hat, even to bed. The kid wants to fix it himself and
gets it wrong: he brings bread, then a healing potion, which only makes Tom groan ("I thought medicine was medicine").
Then he says the things back: the bars, the bed, a golden apple in the priest's window that is "not for sale", the
half price. The cellar is not a cell. Somebody built a room for this. Dad comes down with the apple and a bottle from
the witch outside town, and his hands are shaking, and Tom starts to shake too, red, for minutes, while the sun comes
up the stairs one step at a time. The bigger truth: Dad knows how because it was done for him. So was Mrs Hale, under
the hat. Half the town has knocked twice from that cellar. The rule about the gate is what they tell the kids. Tom
opens his eyes. "He said I owed him a sword."

**Minecraft facts:** a zombie villager is cured with a splash potion of weakness and then a golden apple; it shakes
with red swirls for three to five minutes; iron bars and beds nearby make it quicker; it burns in daylight; a cured
villager sells to you cheaper for good.

**Why it could travel:** a brother is the plainest thing to want back. Many players have cured a villager for the
discount and never thought of it as a family. The viewer who knows the cure sees the cellar for what it is before the
kid does, which is the Monster Village trick.

**To build:** a zombie villager skin (green face, villager nose and robe): small. The cellar: small. The rest is pack.
About 2:20, whole in one film. Night into sunrise, lantern light.

**The risk:** it is the darkest of the five. The first twenty seconds must show Tom is gentle, or it reads as horror.

## 2. *In This Town, My Mum Is a Chicken*

> In this town, every kid had a mum. Mine was a chicken. She walked me to school. I asked her to stop at the corner.

**Frame one:** a row of big zombie mums at the school gate, each holding a small zombie's hand. At the end of the row,
a baby zombie sitting on a chicken, looking at the lens.

**The story.** A zombie town, at night, with a school. The other mums can carry a door. His can lay an egg. He rides
her everywhere, because she will not let him walk ("I was eleven"). She clucks at his teacher. She pecks his
homework. On parents' night she falls asleep on the desk. He tells the other kids she is "a kind of horse". He asked
her once where he came from; she looked at an egg, then at him, and they did not talk about it again. He starts
getting off at the corner. Then the school trip: the cliff road, at the end of the night, with the sun coming. The
bridge goes. The big mums are strong and cannot do a thing about a fall. His mum walks to the edge with him on her
back and steps off. They come down slowly, flapping, past the whole class. She goes back up the long way and does it
again for every kid, one at a time, until the sun is nearly on the rock. The next night, eleven zombie kids want a
ride to school. "I told them to get their own mum." And a tail: there is an egg in her nest, and it is ticking.

**Minecraft facts:** a baby zombie riding a chicken is real and rare (a chicken jockey); chickens flap and take no
damage from a fall, and nor does the rider; chickens lay eggs; zombies burn at sunrise.

**Why it could travel:** being ashamed of your mum at the school gate is something every viewer has done. Frame one is
a picture fans already love. It is the funniest of the five, and the only one with a joke in the title.

**To build:** nothing new of size: `Chicken` and `Zombie` are in the pack; a baby zombie is a scale. A school front and
a cliff: medium. About 1:45, whole in one film.

**The risk:** the nearest of the five to the creeper and enderman films (a mob kid, a mum, the thing he is ashamed of
saves the day). What differs: no players, no raid, and the weakness is hers, not his.

## 3. *In This Town, I Had Never Seen the Sun*

> In this town, I had never seen the sun. Nobody had. We went to bed when it came up. I wanted to see it. Just once.

**Frame one:** a zombie kid at a door, open a hand's width. A blade of gold daylight lies across the floor. His
fingertip is in it, and it is smoking.

**The story.** A zombie town under the trees. The grown-ups call the sun "the bad light". His grandpa calls rain "the
good weather" and nobody knows why. He tries it his own way first: he runs out at dawn and comes back on fire. Then a
dented iron helmet turns up at the edge of town, and the old ones say a hat keeps the light off. He puts it on and
walks out. The film turns from blue to full colour: flowers, bees, a village with its doors open. Over his head, the
helmet's bar, going down. It stays on screen. A villager kid sees him and screams, then sees he is only looking at a
sunflower. They spend the day. The bar is nearly gone and home is far. He runs; the helmet cracks; he stands in a
pond up to his neck with the sun on the water, stuck. Then it clouds over, and it rains, and he walks out of the pond
in broad daylight with nothing on his head. "Now I knew why grandpa called it the good weather." The bigger truth
comes at the edge of the wood: the villager kid has never seen the stars, because his town goes to bed at sunset. So
they meet at the one time both are awake. "He showed me the sun. I showed him the moon." Like and subscribe for part
two: what the villagers did when they found out who he was meeting.

**Minecraft facts:** zombies burn in daylight; one that wears a helmet does not, and the helmet wears out in the sun;
they do not burn in water, in shade or in the rain; villagers go to bed at sunset.

**Why it could travel:** wanting the one thing you are told you cannot have. A bar that runs down on screen (he likes
a clock). The cut from the blue night to the first colour reads with the sound off.

**To build:** a sunflower field and a pond: small. Both towns are pack parts. About 2:10. Can run as parts.

**The risk:** the title sounds like *Nobody Looked at the Moon*. The stories share nothing, but a viewer of both might
think so. Another title: *In This Town, We Went to Bed at Sunrise*.

## 4. *In This Town, My Family Stole Things*

> In this town, my family stole things. My dad took a sword once. My sister took a whole cooked chicken. I was the
> worst thief we ever had.

**Frame one:** three foxes in a row at night on a village roof, each with something in its mouth: a sword, a chicken,
an emerald. The small one at the end has a flower, and is holding it the wrong way up.

**The story.** A fox family in the woods above a village. They sleep all day and work at night. The den is full of
what they took: a bell rope, a hat, eleven left boots. His first job alone: one thing, from one window. He takes the
first thing he sees, a red flower in a cup on a windowsill. In the morning he watches from the bushes as a villager
kid finds the cup empty and cries, because the iron golem gave him that flower. He tries to take it back and it goes
wrong, the way it would: a fox in a village at noon. The golem turns. Dogs. He runs with the flower still in his
mouth. That night there are berries on the windowsill. He drops the flower to eat them, because that is what a fox
does, and goes home with nothing. Dad is not pleased. The next night: berries again, and he has brought a boot; he
drops the boot. He says it back: the berries, the cup, the window left open. "He was not feeding me. He was trading."
The bigger truth: the kid has been getting the town's things back, one a night, for berries. And then the fox finds
the cup on the sill with a new flower in it, and a second cup beside it. Like and subscribe: his dad has seen the
window.

**Minecraft facts:** foxes sleep by day and come into villages at night; a fox carries one thing in its mouth; if it
finds food it drops what it holds to take the food; sweet berries are what they eat; iron golems give poppies to
village children.

**Why it could travel:** a fox with a sword in its mouth is a picture players send each other. The feeling is guilt,
which none of the films so far is about. Frame one has a joke in it before a word is said.

**To build:** a fox (four legs, a tail, a thing in the mouth): new, small, and near the dog of *I Was Told to Sit*.
A den: small. About 1:50.

**The risk:** no talking between the two leads, so faces and bodies carry it. That is what the master prompt asks for,
and it is harder to stage.

## 5. *In This Town, My Parrot Knew First*

> In this town, my grandpa left me a parrot. It could not say hello. It could only say what was coming.

**Frame one:** a kid in bed, sitting bolt upright. On the bedpost a red parrot with its beak wide open, and out of the
beak, drawn in the air, the outline of a creeper. Behind the window, far off, a creeper.

**The story.** Grandpa was the town's watchman, and the parrot sat on his shoulder for forty years. Now it sits on the
kid's. It hisses, and a minute later there is a creeper at the fence. It rattles: a skeleton in the trees. The kid
learns the sounds like a language and writes them down. He rings the bell each time. The first three times the town
comes. By the tenth nobody gets out of bed: "it is the bird again". The baker gives it seeds and calls it a liar.
(Nobody gives it a cookie. "We learned that with the first parrot.") It dances when the kid puts a record on, and
that is the only time it is quiet. Then one evening it makes a sound that is not in his book. Low. Then a horn. He
rings the bell; nobody comes; he runs door to door and gets a door shut on him. He says them back: forty years on
grandpa's shoulder, a book of sounds in grandpa's hand with one page torn out, and the one thing grandpa told him: "if
it ever sounds like a cow that is angry, do not ring the bell. Open the cellar." There is a cellar under the bell
tower, and in it something tall under a sheet. The parrot makes the sound again, and this time it is not the parrot.
Like and subscribe for part two.

**Minecraft facts:** a parrot copies the sounds of monsters within about twenty blocks, higher than the real thing,
and faces the way they are; parrots dance by a jukebox with a record in it; a cookie kills one; a raid comes with a
horn and a ravager.

**Why it could travel:** "nobody believes the kid" is a story everybody knows from the inside. It ends on a
cliffhanger, which he likes, and the world can carry a part two.

**To build:** a parrot (small), a jukebox (small), the bell tower cellar (small). About 2:15 for part one.

**The risk:** the premise is a sound, and the master prompt wants the story to read with the sound off. Each sound
needs a picture: the outline in the air, the page in the book, the monster in the trees.

## The agent's order

1 first: the strongest reason to care, and the viewer is ahead of the teller all the way. Then 3: the clearest wish,
a bar on screen, and the best picture with the sound off. Then 2, the cheapest and the funniest. 4 and 5 after.
