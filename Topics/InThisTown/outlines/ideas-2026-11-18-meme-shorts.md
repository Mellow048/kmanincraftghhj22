# "While you were out partying" shorts — v3, rebuilt on the owner's own reel (2026-11-18)

## 0. What I got wrong, so it does not happen again

I was given two things and I only used one.

1. **A template** — the owner's own reel, *The Guy Who Never Went To Parties*: `HUS HUS HUS` cold open, rewind
   sound, friends shouting at a party, a dream of cooking, an egg on the floor, a training montage, a job at a big
   restaurant, the friends walking in, one word: *"Training."*, catchphrase button.
2. **A tone** — the sigma-grindset meme he asked me to look up, so the funny part is the *sincerity*.

My v2 was a monologue film about a guy catching falling objects. Good meme, **none of his reel**: no party, no
mockery, no failure, no montage, no job, no friends, no one-word punchline. That is why it was not close.

**Rule from here:** the *reel* is the structure. The *meme* is the tone and the one-word punchline. Both, not either.

**What must not move:** the hush, the rewind, the party outside the window, the mess on the floor, the montage,
the oblivious friends, the single word.
**Meme overlay (tone):** his faith is total and the skill is useless. When he says the word, it should sound like a
war veteran answering a polite question.

## 3. A. THE GUY WHO NEVER GOT INVITED — 1:45

Dialogue total: **six lines.** Everything else is sound, faces and hands.

### 1 — `HUS HUS HUS` (0:00–0:04)
Night, outside a house. Music behind a window.
Three friends in the doorway, heads together, whispering hard: **"HUS. HUS. HUS."**
They look at him, once, and go back to whispering.
**Sound:** three dry hushes, close mic, then a low bass thump from the party. No music of our own yet.

### 2 — Rewind (0:04–0:06)
**Sound:** tape rewind, pitch up, cut mid-screech. Picture flips to a hard-cut white frame for two frames.
We are four years earlier. Same doorway, daylight, no party.

### 3 — The party he is not at (0:06–0:14)
Same street, same window. The party is going off: bodies, cups, laughter, someone spilling.
He stands on the pavement with a **borrowed speaker** in both arms, watching through the glass.
**Friend (from the window, cheerful, not cruel):** "Yo — we're full, bro."
He nods. Turns. Walks. **He does not look back once.**

### 4 — The dream (0:14–0:20)
His bedroom. Posters up. One speaker on a chair, one on a plastic crate.
He sits cross-legged between them, headphones on, hands flat on his knees, eyes closed.
**He (quiet, to nobody):** "I want to be a DJ."
Beat. The speaker hums. He nods, once, like it has been agreed.

### 5 — The egg (0:20–0:34)
His cousin's birthday. Fairy lights, plastic chairs, a table with a cake on it. He is set up in the corner with the
borrowed speaker and a borrowed mixer, proud, hands hovering.
Record drops. Two bars play. **Needle skips.**
He panics, grabs a cable, unplugs the wrong one: **feedback squeal** — the whole yard covers their ears.
Silence. Forty faces. A kid, bored, not even mean:
**Kid:** "Put on my phone."
**Sound:** the party restarts from a phone speaker — tiny, tinny, perfect. That is the egg. The mess is that everyone
else's party works better without him.
He coils his cable, alone, while the party happens one metre away.

### 6 — The clean-up (0:34–0:40)
Kitchen, later. He wipes the table. Cake on the floor. Nobody in the room.
He picks up the record, blows on it, puts it back in the sleeve **very** carefully.

### 7 — Training montage (0:40–1:00)
Twelve shots, 1.6 s each, one sound per shot, no dialogue.

| # | Shot | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two records, one hand, drop the needle dead on the beat — first try, wrong | `thud` |
| 2 | Same, hands shaking | `thud` |
| 3 | Hand writing a label: `BPM — 84` | pen |
| 4 | He counts beats while brushing his teeth, head bobbing | brush |
| 5 | Window: night. Two records. Still going | low hum |
| 6 | He mixes with one hand behind his back | `swish` |
| 7 | He mixes with his eyes closed | `swish` |
| 8 | He mixes in the dark, only the mixer lights | `swish` |
| 9 | He tapes a sign to his door: `PRACTICE` | `tape` |
| 10 | Window: dawn. He is asleep sitting up, headphones on | birds |
| 11 | A stack of labelled records, taller than the speaker | `clack` |
| 12 | He drops the needle on a new record and the room *moves* — it is finally right | **music drops in** |

### 8 — The job (1:00–1:12)
The club: the biggest door in town, the one his friends queue at every weekend.
He is behind the decks, headphones round his neck, wearing a black shirt that is too big for him.
The owner leans in, shouting over the bass, and it is the best compliment of his life:
**Owner:** "You're the best I've had."
**He (flat):** "Thank you."
The owner leaves. He lets himself have **one** second of a smile. Then it is gone.

### 9 — They walk in (1:12–1:26)
The three friends from the doorway, four years older, same jackets, same volume, pushing in.
They do not recognise him. They do not even look up at the booth.
One of them leans on the bar and shouts at the ceiling, to nobody:
**Friend:** "Bro, who is this guy? This guy is *sick*."
The other one, shouting at his back, still not looking:
**Friend 2:** "How'd you get this good, man?"
He changes records. He does not turn around.
**Sound:** the room swims, bass drops away to almost nothing, the crowd noise goes distant — the mix goes quiet
just so the word can be heard.

### 10 — The word (1:26–1:34)
Close on his face. Half-lit. He looks at the record, not at them.
**He:** "Bedroom."
**Sound:** needle drop, hard. Music slams back at full volume. They cheer — for the music, not for him, and never
for the answer. He still does not smile.

### 11 — Button (1:34–1:45)
Back outside the house from beat 1. Night. The party behind the glass. The friends whispering.
**"HUS. HUS. HUS."**
He is older now, in the good black shirt, headphones round his neck, walking past them with a crate of records.
He nods at them. They nod back, vaguely, the way you nod at staff.
He keeps walking.
Cut to black on the third hush.
**Caption/CTA:** *"Subscribe if you've ever practised something nobody asked for."*
