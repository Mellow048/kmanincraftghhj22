> # SHORT-FORM SCRIPT, STORYBOARD & CAMERA ENGINE (DEADPAN SURREALIST ESCALATION)
>
> ## 1. IDEATION FORMULA
> Generate concepts by taking a mundane idiom, childhood game, or everyday rule and treating it as a literal,
> inescapable law of society that every character accepts without question:
> - Examples: "Finders keepers" is federal law; "30 minutes or it's free" is a 40-year game of Tag; creditors
>   physically move into your apartment until paid.
> - Protagonist: Relatable first-person ("I") everyman (renter, delivery driver, regular guy).
> - Tone: 100% deadpan and matter-of-fact. Never wink at the camera or call the situation "crazy." Treat surreal
>   events like mild logistical annoyances.
>
> ## 2. SCRIPT CONSTRAINTS
> - Target length: 340–420 words (~1:45 to 2:10 spoken pacing).
> - Sentence style: Short, punchy, active sentences (5–14 words). No "he exclaimed" or "she replied angrily"—weave
>   direct dialogue straight into narration.
> - Specificity: Always use exact numbers, days, and tangible props ($400, 999 deliveries, 7 penguins, Tuesday's
>   sandwich).
>
> ## 3. MANDATORY 6-BEAT STRUCTURE
> 1. IN-MEDIA-RES HOOK (Sentences 1–2): Start mid-action with a mundane act met by an absurd deadpan reaction. Zero
>    intro or greeting.
> 2. RULE & STAKES + CHEKHOV'S GUN (Seconds 8–20): State the governing rule of the world, the ticking clock or
>    stakes, and plant one small physical detail/habit that will be exploited in the climax.
> 3. EXPONENTIAL ESCALATION LADDER (Seconds 20–75): Compound the problem across 5–7 rapid stages using
>    time/countdown anchors ("The next day...", "21 minutes left...", "By Friday...") and escalating locations. Drop
>    one dry, practical one-liner every 2–3 beats.
> 4. THE PIVOT QUESTION (Around 70% mark): Use this exact rhetorical pattern to lock in retention:
>    - Realization: "So that was it. [State the hidden pattern/weakness]."
>    - Pivot: "So how do you [achieve impossible goal against that weakness]? I simply [trigger the planted
>      Chekhov's gun]."
> 5. LOOPHOLE CLIMAX (Seconds 85–105): Resolve the conflict by using the antagonist's own rule, habit, or human
>    reflex against them.
> 6. IRONIC BUTTON ENDING (Final 2–3 sentences): Confirm the main goal succeeded, then immediately hit a punchy twist
>    that loops the premise back around or leaves an ironic consequence.
>
> ## 4. CAMERA, VISUAL & EDITING DIRECTION (VERIFIED FRAME-BY-FRAME)
> - **Visual Style & Expression Snaps:** 3D blocky character models in bright, clean environments with swappable 2D
>   comic/anime facial decals (wide shocked bug-eyes, sweat drops, half-lidded smirks, red rage faces, waterfall
>   tears) that snap instantaneously per beat—no smooth lip-syncing. Add floating 2D comic symbols in 3D space
>   (`Z`s for sleep, yellow `?` for confusion, blue tear droplets for crying).
> - **1-Second Micro-Cut Pacing (95–115 Cuts/Video):** Every single action, noun, or clause gets its own camera angle
>   (0.8–1.4s per shot). Never hold a static shot; keep a subtle continuous handheld drift/float on every frame.
> - **Shot-Reverse-Shot Reaction Loops:** Alternate rapidly between medium close-ups (MCU) of the narrator's
>   deadpan/panicked face and the antagonist's smug/angry face, cutting away to 1-second bystander reaction shots.
> - **Crash Zooms / Snap Push-Ins:** Punch the camera straight into faces and readable props at the end of sentences
>   (e.g., snapping onto a ticking LED timer, a sticky note, a baseball glove, or a written label on an item).
> - **Projectile & Drop-Tracking Shots:** Physically follow key props with the camera when thrown, dropped, or passed
>   (e.g., tracking an item flying through a bus window, keys launching mid-sneeze, a ring bouncing on the floor, or a
>   thrown dinner roll crossing a room).
> - **Whip-Pans & Impact Camera Shake:**
>   - Bridge fast scene or gaze shifts with horizontal/vertical whip-pans and directional motion blur.
>   - Trigger sharp camera jolts/shakes on door knocks, shouts, sneezes, and physical impacts.
> - **Extreme Angle Shifts for Scale & Geometry:**
>   - **Low-Angle Upward Tilts:** Aim up at looming characters (landlord, banker, tax auditor) or tall structures to
>     exaggerate intimidation.
>   - **Top-Down Bird's-Eye Shots:** Cut directly overhead (90 degrees down) to reveal spatial comedy or group
>     geometry instantly (e.g., 40 people packed in a circle passing cash hand-to-hand, or someone taking the bed
>     while the narrator sleeps on the floor).
> - **Front-Facing Chase Tracking & POV/UI Inserts:**
>   - Use "Snorricam"-style front-facing tracking shots locked onto sprinting characters with white radial anime speed
>     lines around the frame edges during chases.
>   - Use first-person POV prop shots (looking down at hands/boxes) and floating 3D smartphone UI screens in
>     center-frame for calls and text twists.
>   - Use a full-screen VHS camcorder overlay (`PLAY ▶`, scanlines, timestamp) for historical flashbacks.
> - **9:16 Framing & 1-Word Captions:** Keep character faces and key props in the upper 70% of the vertical frame.
>   Render subtitles strictly **1 word at a time** in bold, lowercase/sentence-case rounded font with a heavy black
>   outline, alternating **bright yellow and white** in the lower third.

## What that last line means for us

- **Keep what is ours already.** The engine already does one-word captions, blocky avatars, hard cuts, handheld
  drift, camera shake and impact impulses, top-down and low-angle moves, prop-following pans, bloom and grade. Those
  are not rebuilt here; [`SHOTS.md`](../SHOTS.md) maps each idea to the function that already exists.
- **Build only what is missing.** Comic expression decals (partly), floating comic symbols, speed lines, the VHS
  overlay and the phone UI are per-film devices, built in the film's own `kit.tsx` the way the doorway film built its
  doorway.
- **Never copy the reference's stories.** Its plots are Roblox shorts. This topic invents Minecraft premises with a
  Minecraft rule inside them, and uses the reference only for *shape, pace and camera grammar*.
