# Deadpan — the first film of the topic

**Status: an idea, put to the owner 2026-11-25. Nothing built, no script written yet.**

The topic's own work order says the order is: find the rule → write the premise as one line → fill the beat sheet →
count the words → record → build. So this page stops at the premise and the beats, and asks for a yes before the
340–420-word script is written.

## Why this topic, and not another In This Town

Read before writing this: `MASTERPROMPT-01.md`, `AGENTS.md` §0–11 and the thirteen rules, `docs/VIRAL-SHORTS-BLUEPRINT.md`,
`docs/WHY-THE-3-HITS-BLEW-UP.md`, `docs/CAMERA-BIBLE.md`, `docs/DIRECTING.md`, `Topics/README.md`, and the contract
files of all six topics. Three things in there decide where a new idea should go:

1. **Deadpan is the one lane with nothing in it at all.** `Topics/Deadpan/topic.json` says *"No APPROVED film yet.
   The first one becomes the model named here."* There is no Deadpan folder in `src/videos/`, none in `drafts/`, no
   model and no reference page. In This Town has thirteen approved films; Explainer and What If each have one built
   and waiting for him; Stories and Recreation have models. Deadpan has a brief, a contract and no picture.
2. **It is the topic the owner asked for in his own words.** `Topics/Deadpan/reference/OWNER-BRIEF.md`, 2026-11-12:
   *"this is a new topic that i really want to work."* The brief is his, word for word: the six beats, 340–420 words,
   micro-cuts, expression snaps, one-word captions.
3. **In This Town is at the point of collapsing into one shape.** `ideas-2026-11-15.md`: *"ideas 1, 4, 5 and 6 below
   are one outline with four mobs … Do not pitch any of them as new."* Then his verdict on the enderman film: *"kinda
   similar to the creeper one … it might get me in trouble of reused content."* The winning recipe there is
   *odd kid → mob family → players at night → mum's secret*. A sixth film of that shape is the actual risk. Deadpan
   has a different engine (comedy that escalates, not a feeling that turns) and cannot repeat it.

One idea, fully built below, plus two alternates. If he wants a batch of five for this topic, these are the three
that are worth building.

# 1. *The Creeper Broke It. I Bought It.*

> The creeper broke the bakery. The bill came to me. I was five blocks away. I was buying bread.

**One sentence:** In a town where the person a creeper goes off next to pays for the wall, a man is billed six times —
until he gives the creeper a name, an address, and eventually a room in his garden.

**Topic:** `deadpan`. No `cut` line needed: the topic's own numbers (100–132 s) are the default, and its
`short` cut is optional. Six beats, first person, flat.

## Frame one (0.0 s — the first second must contain the premise)

Bright noon. The bakery wall has a creeper-shaped hole in it, smoke still coming off the cobblestone, and a creeper
standing in the hole looking straight at the lens with the engine's `blank` face. In the foreground, the narrator —
`Hero`, one loud colour — holds a paper bill up at chest height. The bill reads **12**. He does not look angry. He
looks like a man reading a bus timetable.

Iconic mob, bright daylight, and a contradiction a viewer can read before a word is said: *the creeper did it, the man
is holding the bill.*

## The rule, and the thing planted inside it

| | |
|---|---|
| The rule | **The nearest-neighbour law.** A creeper only goes off near a person — so whoever it went off nearest pays for the wall. The assessor measures the distance in blocks, and the law bills the nearest **resident**. |
| The clock | The bills come every few days. Each one is bigger than the last, and each one is smaller in blocks. |
| The planted detail | **A name tag in his kitchen drawer. He bought it for a dog he never got.** Placed as a shrug in beat 2. |
| The Minecraft facts | A creeper follows a player and only goes off when it is close to one, so the nearest person really is the reason. Creepers have no money. A **name tag** gives a mob a name; a named mob never despawns and shows its name over its head. |

The trick of the pitch: **the law is a literal reading of a vanilla mechanic**, which is what the blueprint's second
gate asks for. A creeper only explodes near a player, so the town simply bills the nearest person — and it is almost
fair, which is what makes it funny. Escaping it means being far from every creeper, and creepers follow players. The
one word in the law that can save him is *resident*.

### The ladder, stage by stage (the retention engine)

| Stage | Day | Where | Blocks | Bill | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | the bakery | 5 | 12 | he pays, politely |
| 2 | 4 | the well | 4 | 24 | he starts walking in the middle of the road; the creeper follows him, because creepers follow players |
| 3 | 9 | the market | 3 | 60 | he sells his bed; sleeps on the floor |
| 4 | 14 | his own street, 2 a.m., in a thunderstorm | 2 | 96 | he had sold his lamps to pay the last bill, so the creeper got in |
| 5 | 21 | the town hall — while filing a complaint about the law | 1 | 240 | the complaint is assessed; he is billed for the assessor's time |
| 6 | 22 | the assessor's office, with a creeper standing behind him | 0 | the town hall | the pivot |

Every stage is a new place, a smaller number of blocks and a bigger number of emeralds, and stage 4 is *caused* by
stage 3 — he is poor because he pays, and being poor is why his house is dark. That is the whole escalation: the
reaction never changes, the situation does.

### Voice sample (not the script)

> Day four. The well. Four blocks. Twenty-four emeralds.
> Day nine. The market. Three blocks. Sixty emeralds. I sold my bed.
> I could not afford lamps that week. That is how the creeper got in.
> …
> So that was it. The bill never went to the creeper. It went to whoever it went off nearest.
> The law bills the nearest resident. A creeper is not a resident until somebody writes on it.
> I wrote on it.
> …
> Clive had no emeralds. The town took his house.
> His house was the crater.
> …
> It worked. My bills stopped. Clive moved into my garden on the Sunday.
> Named creepers never despawn. It has been two hundred and fourteen days.
> I say good morning. Subscribe if you would say it back.

Numbers are **spoken as words** (the linter prints "spell numbers out so the voice and the captions agree") and
**shown as digits** on the bill prop, which the camera crash-zooms. That is the one design joke of the film: the
voice is calm, the prop is screaming.

## Why it could travel

- **Every player has been billed by a creeper.** The frame-one contradiction is legible with the sound off and needs
  no lore: a hole, a bill, a mob.
- **The running number is the reason to stay.** Twelve, twenty-four, sixty, ninety-six, two hundred and forty. A
  count going up is the cheapest retention device in the blueprint, and it is the topic's own shape.
- **The name tag is the screenshot.** A creeper labelled **CLIVE** with a small brass tag, standing in a garden,
  next to a man saying good morning. That is the frame people post.
- **It gets warm without asking for tears.** The last line is flat and it is the only affectionate one in the film.
  That is the Deadpan version of the emotional flip the three 20k films had; it does not need a mum.
- **Standalone.** No part two. The whole thing is one rule, one man, one creeper.

## Camera (from `SHOTS.md`, using what the engine already has)

- **Every shot:** `handheld: 0.01`. Nothing is static, ever.
- **One action, one angle.** "I sold my bed" is three shots: the bed, the coin stack, the empty floor.
- **The bill is the punchline shot.** A `cam.push` crash zoom ending on the digits — twelve, twenty-four, sixty.
  Three in the film, never more (one per 20 s is the rule).
- **The assessor** is a `Villager` with `smug`, shot from `pitch: -14` at 0.6 m, tape measure in frame, so the
  legal language arrives from below the narrator's eyeline.
- **The tag shot** is the one clean insert in the film: a POV prop insert at `pitch: -30`, hands only, 0.9 s, no
  narration over it. The viewer sees the name before the narrator says it.
- **Top-down** for the seven neighbours all stepping backwards at once as a creeper walks down the street — the
  one group-geometry joke, cut to from a face, never from a wide.
- **Face snaps only on cuts:** narrator `blank` throughout, one `surprised` at the tag, one `hurt` when the bed is
  carried out of the door. The Deadpan rule: panic only reads as panic because the shot before it was flat.

**Four traps in that list, worth knowing before the build:**

- **`Bill`, `Coin`, `CoinStack`, `BillStack`, `Chest` are not in the pack index.** They live in
  `Topics/Stories/money.tsx`, which `index.ts` does not export. This film is bills and emeralds, so it imports
  `../../../Topics/Stories/money` directly, once, in `kit.tsx`. Same for `Bread` in `Topics/Stories/goods.tsx` — the
  loaf in shot one already exists, and `goods.tsx` is not indexed either.
- **`PixelText` is engine, not pack** (`src/engine/blocks.tsx`), so the invoice digits come from there.
- **`Hero` is not a pack part.** Each film defines its own in `kit.tsx` (`Topics/Stories/template/kit.tsx:73`). That
  is where this narrator's loud coat gets decided, once.
- **There is no crowd part.** The queue of neighbours is seven `Villager`s at staggered depths — which is also the
  camera note: stagger depth, not just `x`, or it reads as a row of stickers.

**New, per film, small:** the invoice prop (`PixelText` on a `Block`, crash-zoomed, so it must read at 1080 without
captions); a tape measure; a clipboard and a ledger; the name tag (a brass plate on a strap) and the floating `CLIVE`
label above the creeper; the assessor's office interior. The bakery is `Hut` + `Stall` + `Bread`; the crater is
`Hole` + `Bits` + `Smoke`. That is one new interior and five props, kept in the film's own `kit.tsx` until a second
film asks for them.

## The risks, honestly

1. **No approved Deadpan film exists, so there is no reference to copy.** The camera grammar in `SHOTS.md` is the
   only guide, and the first build will be the topic's reference whether it is good or not. *Mitigation: build and
   measure the first 30 seconds with `scripts/film-metrics.py` before the rest of the sets exist — light, motion,
   cuts a second — and fix the grammar before spending the film.*
2. **Six locations in 108 seconds is the real cost.** Every stage needs a new place or the micro-cuts have nothing
   to cut to. *Mitigation: one street, six fronts on it, plus one interior. The town is a single build.*
3. **The comedy is bureaucracy, and that is a taste.** Nothing explodes after the first shot; the rest is measuring,
   billing and paperwork. That is the genre's engine, and the brief's own example (creditors physically moving in) is
   the same joke. If he wants more bang per beat, `Boom` can go off at the head of each stage — one prop,
   six placements, maybe eight extra shots, no new sets.
4. **One creeper as a housemate can look like the monster-family films.** What separates it: no family, no night
   raid, no secret, and the creeper never does anything kind. The joke is administrative. If he still finds
   it too close, alternates 2 and 3 below have no mob in them at all.

# Alternates, if the first one is not the one

**2. *No Refunds.*** — the shop's returns policy is literal: a returned thing must go back to the exact block it came
from. He returns a door; the hole is measured; he carries the door across town at three in the morning to put it back.
Ladder: a door, a bucket of water (it will not stay where he puts it — water flows), a chicken (it wanders off the
receipt), and finally the shop's own floor. Loophole: he returns the shop's floorboards, so the shop is legally
outdoors. Button: the shop now has a queue round the block, because it is the only shop with no walls. *Mechanic:
block placing, water flow, mob pathing. No mob lead; the whole cast is villagers.*

**3. *You Can't Take It With You.*** — the town's inheritance law: when you die you drop everything, and whoever picks
it up first keeps it. At his father's wake, forty-one relatives stand over the grave with their hands out. Ladder: the
will, the probate queue, the cousin with a hopper (a machine that picks items up first, legally), the auction of a
dead man's boots. Loophole: an **ender chest** — contents survive death, so the estate was never on him. Button: the
estate is saved, and the tax office bills him for the chest, because the chest is now the only thing that inherits.
*Mechanic: death drops, item despawn, hoppers, ender chests. Darker than the channel's usual; no mobs at all.*
