# Five films — pitched against part one

Five pitches, all "What if", all built to [`STUDY.md`](../STUDY.md) and [`RETENTION.md`](../InThisTown/RETENTION.md).
The owner said: no script review, automatic mode, **one film at a time** — make it, inspect it, review it, polish it,
one last polish, then the next. The order below is the order they get made.

Written 2026-11-16. Each one has to pass the part-1 test that matters: **five jokes before 0:20, the rule readable in
twelve words, the hole is about a person, the teller is doing the thing.**

---

## 1. What If You Could See How Long You Had — **BUILT**

`src/videos/what-if-you-could-see-how-long-you-had/`

| | |
|---|---|
| Rule (12 words) | A number over every head: the seconds until that person snaps. |
| The hole | Jo has no number at all — until he talks to her, and it says one. |
| The five jokes | a boy at four; a teacher at forty; a kid told "you have ten seconds, get lost"; two clocks he did not expect; his own head in the mirror at ten |
| The price | He stops talking to people, to keep their numbers up. Which is what puts Jo at one. |
| The misread he says out loud | "I thought it meant how long they had left." |
| First payoff (36%) | The teacher, at forty, does not go off; going off is not what the number is. |
| Promise paid by picture (57%) | Jo, with a 1 over her head, standing where a girl with nothing over her had been. |
| Last line | "It was never counting down. It was counting on me." |

## 2. What If Your Shadow Did What You Were Thinking

| | |
|---|---|
| Hook | "What if your shadow did what you were thinking, instead of what you were doing?" |
| Rule (12 words) | My shadow moves on its own, and it always tells the truth. |
| The hole | His shadow visits one house every night that he has never been to. |
| The five jokes | the shadow waving at a girl he is not waving at · the shadow sitting down while he stands at assembly · the shadow fist-fighting nobody · the shadow copying the teacher, badly · the shadow asleep while he is being shouted at |
| The price | Everybody can read him, so he learns to think nothing — and then his shadow stops moving at all. |
| The misread | "I thought my shadow was the liar." |
| First payoff (36%) | The shadow is not copying him; it is going somewhere, and he can follow it. |
| Promise paid (57%) | The shadow is standing at a bedroom window where a kid is crying, and it is waving. |
| Threat (40%) | He starts being unable to control it — it walks into traffic, into a fight, into somebody's house. |
| The reveal | The shadow is his brother's. His brother died; his shadow never left. |
| Last line | "My shadow was not following me. I was following it." |

**Why it can hold 82%:** the shadow is a free special effect that costs nothing, and it is funny in every single shot
it is in. The hole is a person, and it hurts.

## 3. What If Everybody Forgot You The Moment You Left The Room

| | |
|---|---|
| Hook | "What if everybody forgot you the moment you left the room?" |
| Rule (12 words) | I leave a room, and every person in it forgets me. |
| The hole | One girl remembers, and she will not say how. |
| The five jokes | free food, because the canteen lady forgets he paid · a teacher who has told him off four times in a row · his mum setting a fifth place for him every night · a whole class looking at an empty chair, terrified · a fight he wins by walking out and back in |
| The price | He can do anything, and nothing he does counts. |
| The misread | "I thought it was the best thing that ever happened to me." |
| First payoff (36%) | It is not a power he has; it is a thing that was done **to** him, and somebody did it on purpose. |
| Promise paid (57%) | He walks out and comes back, and the girl is still looking at the door. |
| Threat (40%) | Somebody is using his power: they wait outside rooms. |
| The reveal | His mum is the one paying to keep him forgotten. She has been keeping him safe from something. |
| Last line | "Nobody forgot me. Somebody made sure of it." |

## 4. What If Every Door On Your Street Opened Somewhere Else

| | |
|---|---|
| Hook | "What if every door on your street opened somewhere else?" |
| Rule (12 words) | The door you pick decides where you come out. |
| The hole | One door in one house opens into the same room every time, and he has never seen it from the inside. |
| The five jokes | going to school through the fridge · the neighbour coming out of his own wardrobe · a dare that ends in a swimming pool · his dad walking out of a door into a wall · a pizza that arrives through the wrong door twenty minutes late |
| The price | He stops using the street. Every shortcut costs somebody else something. |
| The misread | "I thought I was the only one who knew which door was which." |
| First payoff (36%) | Somebody has been mapping the doors too, and the map is his own handwriting. |
| Promise paid (57%) | The one door that always opens the same place: it is his room, from the outside, with him in it. |
| Threat (40%) | He opens a door and something comes through that he did not send for. |
| Last line | "I did not find the doors. The doors were waiting for somebody who would keep count." |

## 5. What If You Could Hear The Last Thing A Place Remembered

| | |
|---|---|
| Hook | "What if you could hear the last thing a place remembered?" |
| Rule (12 words) | I touch a wall, and I hear the last voice it heard. |
| The hole | Every wall in his house remembers the same voice, and it is not his family's. |
| The five jokes | a bench that says "ow" · a classroom wall that remembers the teacher's bad joke · a bus stop that says the same sentence every day · a wall that remembers him saying something he did not say · the gym wall, which remembers nothing, and is the quietest place in town |
| The price | He stops touching things, and then he cannot sit still or hold anybody's hand. |
| The misread | "I thought walls remembered the important things." |
| First payoff (36%) | A wall remembers a voice saying his name before he was born. |
| Promise paid (57%) | The wall of his own room, at the end of the film, remembering a version of the story he was not in. |
| Threat (40%) | Something in the house starts talking back to him. |
| Last line | "The house was not remembering. It was waiting for me to say it back." |

## The order, and why

1. **One Clock** — done. (The number is the power and the power is a rule.)
2. **Your Shadow** — the funniest, and the power is a picture in every shot. Best chance of holding the audience.
3. **Everyone Forgets You** — the strongest premise of the five, and the hardest to stage. Third, when there is time.
4. **Every Door** — the most visual; the school is a set for it already.
5. **Hear The Last Thing** — the quietest; sound carries it, which is riskier, so it goes last.
