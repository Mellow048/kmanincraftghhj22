# What part one actually does — studied from the film, not from the notes

**Why this file exists.** "In This Town, I Live in a Monster Village" (part one) reached **82% stayed to watch**, the
highest of any film we have made. The owner asked for it to be studied properly — the script, the hook, the sound,
the idea — and then for five films built to it. This is that study, and the checklist every one of the five is held
to. It is not a summary of [`RETENTION.md`](../InThisTown/RETENTION.md): that file is the numbers. This is the
mechanism underneath them, read off the script, the timing and the sound sheet.

Anything marked **(measured)** is a number taken from the files in
`src/videos/in-this-town-monster-village/`. Anything marked **(judgement)** is what I think the number means.

---

## 0. The whole film in one line

**A boy who is not a monster spends two minutes and fifty seconds being quietly wrong about why the monsters are
being kind to him.**

Every single beat is that sentence said again in a new way. The premise, the jokes, the locked door, the ring, the
bread, the sneeze, the ending: all of it is the same small "oh no" repeated with better and better information.
Nobody in the film ever changes subject.

---

## 1. The hook — the first 18 seconds

**(measured)** Seven shots, 18.37 seconds, 62 words:

| t | line | words | seconds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | In this town, I was the only one who was not a monster. | 13 | 2.73 |
| 2.73 | My mum was a zombie. | 5 | 1.07 |
| 3.80 | My dad was a skeleton. | 5 | 1.23 |
| 5.03 | And I was a boy they found in a basket. | 9 | 1.77 |
| 6.80 | Our neighbour Hiss was a creeper. He loved hugs. Nobody wanted one. | 12 | 3.43 |
| 10.23 | The school bus was a slime. You got in. You got out wet. | 12 | 2.87 |
| 13.10 | Every monster stayed out of the sun. The sun burned them. | 11 | 2.60 |

What each of the seven is doing:

1. **The odd sentence.** "I was the only one who was not a monster" is a normal sentence with one word wrong in it.
   That is the whole hook technique, and it is worth naming because it is reusable: *say a familiar thing and put
   one impossible noun in it.* Not "there were monsters in my town". The viewer's correction of the sentence is the
   thing that keeps them.
2. **Two one-second jokes.** Mum, dad. Five words each. **(measured)** 1.07 s and 1.23 s, the two fastest shots in
   the film, at the point where most films are still setting up. They are not information, they are *rhythm*: the
   film demonstrates in its first four seconds that it will not waste a second.
3. **The title's promise, in the third joke.** "A boy they found in a basket" is the one line in the hook that is
   actually about him. It looks like a joke and it is the plant that pays at 2:29 ("Who do you think carried the
   basket here?").
4. **The escalation.** Hiss is one joke with three beats inside it (loved hugs / nobody wanted one). The bus is the
   same joke shape one notch more absurd. **(measured)** these two are the longest hook shots — 3.43 s and 2.87 s —
   because they are the only two with a *scene* in them rather than a fact.
5. **The rule that makes him special.** Sun burns monsters. He is the only one who can stand in it. This is the hook
   paying off the first line, and it is also the setup for the meeting at 0:18.

**The shape:** odd sentence → two 1-second jokes → one soft plant → two bigger jokes → the rule. Seven shots. The
viewer has had five laughs and one question and is 18 seconds in.

**(judgement) The single most important thing about this hook is that it is FUNNY FIRST.** The mystery is not
introduced until 0:24. The first 24 seconds are a comedy, and the audience commits to a comedy. A film that opens
with a mystery asks the viewer to do work before it has given them anything. This is the mistake I would make if I
were not careful, and it is the mistake part 4 made.

## 2. The idea — why this premise can hold 82%

**(judgement)** Four properties, and a What If film needs all four:

1. **The power is a RULE, said in twelve words or fewer, and the viewer can apply it themselves.** "I see a clock
   over your head, and the number is how long you have." A viewer who can apply the rule starts predicting, and a
   viewer who is predicting is a viewer who is still there. Part 1's rule is "the sun burns monsters, not me" — the
   viewer can apply that to every frame.
2. **The rule has a HOLE, and the hole is the story.** Part 1: the sun burns monsters, so why can Mo sit in it? The
   hole is not a plot twist bolted on at the end; it is visible from the first time the rule is stated.
3. **The hole is about a PERSON, not a mechanic.** "It is the hat" is funny for ten seconds and then it is not,
   because the real answer is "Mo is human". A rule with a hole that resolves into a *person* is a story. A rule
   with a hole that resolves into a *mechanic* (it only works at night / you have to say it three times) is a
   Wikipedia article.
4. **The premise can be photographed cheaply and repeatedly.** **(measured)** Part 1 says the same thing in every
   scene: a small figure in yellow among big monsters. It never needs a new set, a new effect, or a new camera
   trick. The five films have to be like this or they cannot be made fast.

## 3. The spine — part 1's beats re-derived for a power film

**(measured)** positions, as a percentage of a 175 s film, plus what a What If film does at that position.

| % | part 1 | part 1 does | A What If film does here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | "I was the only one who was not a monster" | odd sentence, one picture | the title question, then "One morning, I could." |
| 1.5–9 | mum / dad / basket / Hiss / bus | **five jokes, 1–3 s each** | five things the power does, each a joke, each < 3 s |
| 10 | "Then there was Mo" | the other one arrives | the one person the power cannot read |
| 14 | "He just looked at me. A bit too long." | **first question out loud** | the first thing that does not fit |
| 19–27 | bread / lantern / sneeze | clues, **each one funny** | three more oddities, each one funny |
| 27 | "I never asked myself why" | **the question named** | the film names what it does not know |
| 31 | "They locked my door. From the outside." | **misdirection** | the power reads as a gift for 10 s |
| 36 | "...and they were facing out." | **first payoff / reversal** | the first true thing the power shows him |
| 40 | Players, with swords | **threat, change of gear** | the price arrives and it can hurt someone |
| 57 | Red blood | **the promise paid with a picture** | the title's question, answered in one image |
| 65 | "I am human too" | the line the thumbnail sold | the line the film was built for |
| 69 | "Who do you think carried the basket here?" | the opening line, re-read | line 1 now means something else |
| 74 | "They had come for him" | reversal two | the power was never about the teller |
| 77 | "Hug." | comic payoff, one word | one word ends the threat |
| 86–88 | the hat goes back on → an army | acceptance, then a new stair | he is forgiven, then a bigger thing appears |
| 99 | iron armour, "subscribe for part two" | cliffhanger then the ask | the ask, with the next question already open |

**Three structural facts, and they are non-negotiable:**

- **The first payoff lands at 36%, not at 90%.** A viewer who gets a real answer a third of the way in stops
  bracing for a film that will not deliver.
- **Every clue is a joke.** Bread, lantern, sneeze, sun. **(judgement)** If a clue is not funny it is an
  explanation, and explanations are where people leave.
- **The last quarter escalates twice.** Acceptance at 86%, army at 88%. The film refuses to end on a warm shot.

## 4. The script — nine habits, with the line that proves each

1. **One clause per idea, and the verb at the front.** "He just looked at me. A bit too long." Two sentences, ten
   words, a whole relationship. **(judgement)** The rhythm is short-short-short-LONG; the long one is the one that
   lands the beat.
2. **The teller is the one doing things.** **(measured)** He hides, runs, stands, says, shuts his eyes. Part 4 said
   "I did not see that either" twice and lost the owner at 1:05.
3. **Direct speech, roughly every 15 seconds.** **(measured)** 10 spoken lines in 74 shots; part 4 had 6, four of
   them the teller alone. "It is the hat." "Monsters! Get them all!" "Hug." "I am human too." "Zombies do not eat
   bread."
4. **People answer each other, and the answers are jokes.** The whole village's kindness is explained by a joke
   about bread. **(judgement)** The best line in the film is the one that turns the mystery into a joke, because it
   pays the plot and the comedy at the same time.
5. **Every plant is said twice, and the second time is funnier.** The basket, at 0:05 and 2:29. The hat, at 0:19
   and 2:22. **(judgement)** Plants are not hidden; they are repeated until the viewer remembers them.
6. **Nothing is explained that the viewer can work out.** Mo's hand bleeding red is the reveal; nobody says "he was
   human all along" until Mo says it, in six words, as a joke.
7. **The teller is wrong out loud.** "I thought he did not like humans." "I thought it was because I was the weak
   one." Every misread is stated so the reveal can correct a *sentence the viewer remembers*.
8. **The last line of a section is a question or a door.** "I never asked myself why." "Then I saw why." "So I ran."
   **(judgement)** These are the joinery. A section that ends on a full stop is a place to leave.
9. **The ask is in the story's voice.** "Like and subscribe for part two" arrives on the same line as the suit of
   armour. It is the answer to "what happens now", not a request bolted on.

## 5. The sound — what the sheet actually looks like

**(measured)** 179 effects in 175 s = **61.4 a minute**, 23 music sections, 9 beds.

- **157 of 179 effects sit on a spoken word, not on the cut.** This is the single biggest technical difference
  between part 1 and a film that feels like a slideshow. "Mum *was a zombie*" → blip. "Into the *slime*" → splash.
  "Spat *out*" → boing. The picture cuts on the line; the sound lands on the **word inside** the line. That is what
  makes it feel dubbed by a person rather than assembled.
- **Loud one-shots never land on a word the teller is speaking, unless they are the point.** **(measured)** 47 of
  the loud effects are on words, and every one of them is a *sting on the reveal word* ("Mo" → sting, "red" →
  drop). The rest of the word-anchored effects are quiet: pop, blip, tick, plink, rustle, splash, boing.
- **23 music sections, not 5.** **(measured)** The mood changes every 6–9 seconds for the first minute: town →
  curious → sneak → playful → hope → curious → sneak → drone → tense. **(judgement)** This is the real engine of
  pace. The cuts are 2.3 s; the music changes are 7 s; so the viewer's ear gets a new room twice as often as the
  eye gets a new shot, and the two never change together.
- **Silence is used once, and it is a beat.** "It went quiet" → `none`. The music stops for the one line where the
  world stops.
- **Music sits 16 dB under the voice; beds at −26 to −32 dB.** **(measured)** You never hear the bed, you only miss
  it when it is gone.

For a 90-second What If film that means: **≥ 60 effects** (40/min is the lint floor; part 1's rate is 61), **≥ 10
music sections** (not 5), and **most effects on words**.

## 6. The picture — what makes it cheap and what makes it good

**(judgement, from reading scene1–scene10):**

- **One silhouette the viewer can recognise at a glance.** Yellow boy. In a What If film that is the power's
  marker: one look, repeated, never changing.
- **A new framing every shot, and a camera that is always moving.** **(measured)** 74 shots, median 2.37 s, no
  locked-off frames.
- **The joke is in the staging, not only in the words.** The monsters pop up ONE AT A TIME behind him on a timed
  stagger (0.25 s apart). Hiss is placed at the far end so "nobody wanted one" is spatially true.
- **Every scene has an environment shot, a travelling shot, an odd angle and a close-up.**

## 7. The part-4 failure, in one table — this is the trap

| what part 4 did | why it lost 20 points |
|---|---|
| Two lines of set-dressing before anything was owed | the first 10 s gave the viewer nothing to hold |
| Three lines about a guard the viewer does not know | the teller became a camera |
| "I did not see any of this. They told me later." | the film disowned its own footage |
| 36 seconds of the teller watching somebody else | 1:05, where the owner stopped |
| A flashback after the payoff it explains | the film spent its middle re-explaining itself |

**(judgement) The one-sentence test for every shot I write:** *is the teller in it, doing the thing the line says?*
If the answer is "he is watching", the shot is wrong.

## 8. The checklist the five films are built to

Copied from what part 1 measurably does, not from the lint.

**The script**
- [ ] Line 1 is a normal sentence with one impossible word in it, and it starts "What if".
- [ ] **Five jokes before 0:20.** Count them. Fewer than five is not this film.
- [ ] The power's rule is stated in ≤ 12 words and the viewer can apply it in the first 30 s.
- [ ] The rule has a hole, the hole is about a person, and the film says the hole out loud by 30%.
- [ ] A misread the teller says out loud ("I thought…"), so the reveal can correct a sentence.
- [ ] Somebody other than the teller speaks at least every 15 s.
- [ ] Every plant is said twice; the second time is funnier.
- [ ] First real payoff by 40%. Promise paid with a picture by 60%.
- [ ] The last quarter escalates twice, then asks.
- [ ] No line of "I did not see this".

**The sound**
- [ ] ≥ 10 music sections, a new mood every 6–9 s for the first minute.
- [ ] ≥ 60 effects, and **most of them on a word, not on the cut**.
- [ ] Loud effects only on reveal words; quiet ones everywhere else.
- [ ] One stretch of no music at the beat where the world stops.

**The picture**
- [ ] One look for the power, identical every time, big enough to read on a phone.
- [ ] The teller is doing the thing in most shots.
- [ ] New framing every shot; median 2.0–2.4 s.
- [ ] Every scene: environment, travelling, odd angle, close-up.

**The handover**
- [ ] Measured timing, built mix, `npm run check` PASS.
- [ ] The mp4 in `renders/`.
- [ ] Say that the sound was measured, never heard.
