# The Stories reference, studied — "His Dad Kept Punching The Same Tree For An HOUR.."

Studied from `reference for new topic/`: the 67-second mp4 frame by frame (23 frames at 3-second steps), the note
files and screenshots, and the tactiq transcript. This page is what a new film of this topic must copy.

Source: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJJioNVCRqI`

## Specs
9:16, 1080×1920, **67 s**, 3D Minecraft machinima, warm shaders (sunlight, soft shadows, bloom), rigid block rigs
with no bent joints. Cuts every **1–2 s** across wide, medium and macro angles. Captions: bold uppercase blocky
pixel font, white with a black outline, **1–3 words at a time**, centre screen, synced to the narration. In-world
dialogue uses dark translucent nametags above the speaker.

## Three sets, two characters, one conflict
- **Set A, forest:** oak tree on grass, crafting table, chest, bright sky.
- **Set B, bedroom:** oak planks, green rug, PC desk with monitor, bench under a 2×2 window that changes day to night.
- **Set C, cottage:** the finished house, soul lantern, flower box — the payoff location, seen for four seconds.
- **Dad:** adult rig, black hair, brown beard, wide-open mouth, blue shirt with a white chest stripe, grey pants.
- **Tom:** child rig, nametag `Tom`, brown hair, blushing cheeks, orange hoodie, blue pants.

## What makes it work (and what to copy)
1. **Two engines running at once:** a mechanical joke (click vs hold) and an emotional debt (a promise to a kid). The
   joke gets the first ten seconds, the debt carries the rest. Monster Village did the same with a rule and a secret.
2. **The impossible claim is answered within seconds.** "Punched the same tree for an hour" is resolved by "clicking
   resets the crack". Never leave a viewer confused for more than one line.
3. **Time-cards, not montages.** "1 HOUR LATER", "3 SECONDS LATER" compress time in four words and read instantly.
4. **Sound carries the turn.** Punch/UI/shatter SFX all the way; music is a muted synth pulse at the open, warm
   strings at 0:22, **full silence at 0:45**, then soaring strings 0:51 to the end. The silence is the joke's funeral.
5. **Captions are the rhythm.** One to three words, always the spoken words, never over a face.
6. **Three sets, reused.** No location is invented for variety; variety comes from angle, distance and light.
7. **The CTA is thematic.** "Subscribe if you'd never give up." It closes the story instead of interrupting it.

## What is different from Monster Village Part 1
67 s against 175 s. Two characters against a village. One small promise against a war. But the same bones: a
premise stated as a fact in the first second, a rule the hero gets wrong, a child who sees the truth before the
adult does, a low point with the music pulled out, and a last line that asks for the subscribe *because of* the story.

## What this topic's films must therefore be
Short (60–120 s), 2–3 sets, 2–4 characters, one mechanic and one debt, a visible time-card, silence at the low
point, a CTA that names the theme. Write the mechanic so it can be understood from a single frame, and the debt so
it can be understood from a single line.
