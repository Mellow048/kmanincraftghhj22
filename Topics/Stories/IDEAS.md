# Stories — five ideas, built to the reference's engine

The reference works on two engines at once: a game mechanic the audience already knows, and a debt owed to a
child. The mechanic is the joke in the first second. The debt is why anyone stays for the last.

Rules every pitch here obeys: the impossible fact lands in the first second, the mechanic is legible from one
frame, the debt is legible from one line, three sets at most, two or three characters, and the payoff is
something you can *see* happen.

---

## 1. "My Dad Hasn't Left The Doorway In Six Years"
**Hook:** My dad hasn't left our doorway in six years. Not because he's stuck. Because monsters can't walk
through a player.

**Mechanic:** In Minecraft, a mob will not path through a one-block gap with a player standing in it. He found
that out on night one and never moved again.

**The debt:** Six years of a man standing still while his son's world got bigger behind him. The boy doesn't
know what it cost until he sees the dad's inventory: nothing. No axe, no food, no bed. He's been logged in for
six years with an empty bag.

**Sets:** the doorway (interior, warm), the yard at night (moon, zombies outside the light), the world behind
him (a garden the boy built, which the dad has never seen).

**Payoff:** The boy stands in the doorway for one night. The dad walks into his own garden for the first time.
Last line: *"Subscribe if you'd take a shift."*

**Why it's cheap:** one doorway, three camera distances, one night. Light is the whole design.

---

## 2. "My Dad Has Died Four Hundred Times"
**Hook:** My dad has died four hundred times in this game. He's not bad at it. He's paying.

**Mechanic:** Dying is free. You respawn at the bed. So a man who has nothing else to give will spend deaths
instead of diamonds.

**The debt:** The boy dropped something in the lava — something that can't be crafted again — and the dad has
been walking back into the same cave, over and over, to reach it. The death counter is the only thing he ever
levels up.

**Sets:** the bedroom (bed = respawn point), the cave mouth, the lava shore.

**Payoff:** The boy stops him, and instead of saving the item, breaks the bed so his father can't come back to
that cave. Last line: *"Subscribe if you'd break the bed too."*

**Why it's cheap:** a cave mouth, a lava glow, a counter card. The engine already has all three.

---

## 3. "My Dad Turned Off The Monsters"
**Hook:** My dad turned off the monsters when I was six. He didn't know he was turning off the sky.

**Mechanic:** Peaceful mode: no hostile spawns — and no phantoms, no endermen, no night sounds. The world goes
quiet and safe and grey.

**The debt:** He did it because the boy screamed at a zombie. But safety, kept too long, is a smaller world.
The dad has never seen the boy fight anything, and the boy has never seen his dad afraid.

**Sets:** the same bedroom in two modes (lit and humming vs empty and silent), the field at night, the village
square.

**Payoff:** The boy switches it back on, at night, with his father standing next to him. First mob appears;
the dad's hands shake; they stand anyway. Last line: *"Subscribe if you'd turn it back on."*

**Why it's cheap:** one set shot twice, plus one field. The mode change is the entire visual effect.

---

## 4. "My Dad Still Plays The World I Deleted"
**Hook:** My dad still plays in a world I deleted three years ago.

**Mechanic:** Old saves. The boy moved on to a new world, new seeds, new friends. The dad stayed in the file
from the summer the boy was nine, because that is where the base is.

**The debt:** The boy deleted the world; the dad restored it from a backup he never told anyone he kept.

**Sets:** the old base (the first night's dirt hut, now surrounded by everything the dad built alone), the
boy's new world (stone, glass, neat), the desktop at night.

**Payoff:** The boy logs into the old world — the hut is now a house, and there's a second bed in it, made
years ago, never slept in. Last line: *"Subscribe if you'd log back in."*

**Why it's cheap:** two bases, one desk. The old base can be built from dirt and planks in ten minutes.

---

## 5. "My Dad Only Owns One Torch"
**Hook:** My dad owns one torch. He carries it, places it, picks it up, and carries it again.

**Mechanic:** A torch has light radius. A man who carries his only torch lives inside a circle he can move,
and the rest of the world stays dark.

**The debt:** He is not poor. He gave every other torch to the boy's friends so they could find their way home,
and kept one.

**Sets:** the road at night, the friends' houses (lit windows), the dark between them.

**Payoff:** The boy walks out with a torch of his own and puts it down next to his father's — two circles
overlapping. Last line: *"Subscribe if you'd walk him home."*

**Why it's cheap:** torch light on a night road is the best-looking thing this engine does.

## What has to change before the first one

`Topics/Stories/topic.json` was written before the reference arrived. It says 30–58 seconds, and its camera
rule is a continuous flight with at most two hard cuts. The reference is **67 seconds and cuts every 1–2
seconds**, and it is the reason the topic exists. So: length to 55–75 s, shots 0.8–3.5 s, cuts allowed and
expected, and the CTA stays the last spoken line.
